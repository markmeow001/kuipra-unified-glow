Ask five marketing agencies which platform your business needs and you'll get the same answer five times: all of them. That answer is great for agency retainers and terrible for a small business with one person doing marketing off the side of their desk.

Here's the truth we tell clients on day one: for most Vancouver businesses, the right number of platforms to start with is one. The real question is which one — and that's a decision you can make systematically, not by vibes.

## The question is never "which platform is best"

Platforms don't have winners. They have audiences and mechanics. The only question that matters is: **where does *your* customer discover businesses like yours, and can you sustain the content that platform rewards?**

Get that match right on one platform and you'll outperform competitors who post half-heartedly on three. Get it wrong and no amount of posting volume saves you.

## What each platform actually rewards

### Instagram: consistency and local visual identity

Instagram discovery runs on aesthetic coherence plus local signals. A grid that looks like one brand, Reels and Stories that reinforce it, and disciplined use of location tags and Vancouver-area hashtags — that's what compounds. Instagram is the closest thing to a digital storefront: people who hear about you elsewhere come here to check whether you're legit. That makes it the strongest default for visually-driven businesses, and a near-mandatory *secondary* presence for everyone else.

Two newer platform rules matter for local businesses. Instagram now allows at most five hashtags on a post ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/351460621611097)), so pick a few specific local tags instead of pasting a block of thirty. And photos and videos from public posts and reels on a public professional account, held by someone over 18, can be indexed by Google and Bing ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/147542625391305)). That makes plain captions that name the dish or service and the neighbourhood worth the extra ten seconds.

### TikTok: the hook, then everything else

TikTok doesn't care what your grid looks like. It cares whether the first two seconds of a video stop the scroll, and its recommendation engine will show a good video from a zero-follower account to thousands of people. Just as important: younger customers increasingly *search* TikTok the way older ones search Google — "best brunch Vancouver," "Kitsilano hair salon." If your customer is under 30 and your business has personality, process, or transformation to show on camera, TikTok offers the highest ceiling — and demands the most ongoing creative energy. Our [no-studio short-form video guide](/blog/short-form-video-guide-vancouver/) covers formats a two-person team can keep up, and the same vertical clips work on Reels and Xiaohongshu.

If the 2024 headlines made you wary, here is where things stand. In November 2024 the federal government ordered TikTok Technology Canada to wind up its Canadian business after a national security review, while stating it was not blocking Canadians' access to the app ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2024/11/government-of-canada-orders-the-wind-up-of-tiktok-technology-canada-inc-following-a-national-security-review-under-the-investment-canada-act.html)). In January 2026 the Federal Court set that order aside with the consent of both the government and TikTok ([CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/01/22/federal-government-agreed-to-set-aside-order-to-wind-down-tiktok-in-canada/)). On March 9, 2026, after a new review, the government allowed TikTok Canada to keep operating, subject to legally binding undertakings: new security gateways for Canadians' personal information, stronger protections for minors, and an independent third-party monitor auditing data access ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/03/minister-jolys-statement-on-the-outcome-of-the-further-national-security-review-of-tiktok-technology-canada-inc-under-the-investment-canada-act.html)). We have found no further order since. Pick TikTok, or don't, based on your customers rather than the headlines. Either way, don't let a platform you rent be the only place you can reach people: an [email list](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/) is yours.

### Xiaohongshu (RED): authenticity for Chinese-speaking Vancouver

RED is where Chinese-speaking Metro Vancouver — a market we've written about [in depth](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/) — researches almost every local purchase. Its mechanics reward the opposite of advertising: detailed, honest, review-style notes that read like a friend's recommendation. Polished brand content underperforms; genuine customer experiences travel. If a meaningful share of your customers speak Chinese, RED isn't optional — restaurants especially, as we covered in our [Xiaohongshu restaurant guide](/blog/xiaohongshu-restaurant-marketing-vancouver/), but the same logic applies to beauty, retail, real estate, and education. If you're weighing whether to run it in-house or hire help, our [full Vancouver Xiaohongshu guide](/blog/xiaohongshu-marketing-vancouver/) breaks down what each path actually costs.

And if you're wondering where WeChat fits: it isn't competing for this slot. RED is where Chinese-speaking customers find you; WeChat is where you keep in touch with them afterwards, as our [WeChat guide for Canadian businesses](/blog/wechat-marketing-canadian-business/) explains.

## Match the platform to your business type

- **Visual retail, food, beauty, experiences** — your product photographs and films well. Instagram or RED first, depending on your customer's language; TikTok if you have someone comfortable on camera.
- **Service businesses** (trades, clinics, consultants, real estate) — trust matters more than aesthetics. Instagram for credibility-building; RED if you serve the Chinese community, where service categories perform strongly; TikTok only with a genuine on-camera personality.
- **B2B and professional services** — honestly, often none of the three as a primary channel. LinkedIn and search usually beat all of them. Keep a minimal Instagram as a legitimacy check, and don't force short-form video because a competitor did.

## The worksheet: three questions in order

1. **Who is your customer?** Age, language, neighbourhood. Be specific — "everyone in Vancouver" is not an answer.
2. **Where do they discover businesses like yours?** Not where they scroll for fun — where they look when they're deciding. Ask ten real customers how they found you; the pattern is usually obvious.
3. **What content can you sustain weekly?** Not in a motivated week — every week, indefinitely. If nobody on your team will film, TikTok is out no matter what the demographics say. Choose the platform whose native format matches what you can actually produce, and test it against a [content calendar a small team can sustain](/blog/content-calendar-for-small-business/) before you commit.

Where all three answers intersect, that's your platform. If two platforms survive the test, pick the one where question three feels easiest — sustainability wins ties.

## One done well beats three done badly

An abandoned account is worse than no account: it's public evidence of neglect, sitting exactly where potential customers go to evaluate you. Commit to one platform for six months. Add a second only when the first runs on routine, drives measurable enquiries, and you've identified an audience it structurally can't reach — that's when a RED-first restaurant adds Instagram for its English-speaking crowd, or an Instagram-first brand adds TikTok to get in front of Gen Z.
