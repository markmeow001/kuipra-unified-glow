At two in the morning, most of Vancouver's cafés have been dark for hours. Breka is not.

Every one of its locations stays open through the night. Not just on weekends, not just during exam season: 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year, as the company's [About page](https://www.breka.ca/about-us) puts it. Local coverage was already listing its shops among the city's 24-hour spots [a decade ago](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/24-hour-places-in-vancouver-for-late-night-studying). On its website, "Open 24/7" sits in the main navigation, where most businesses would put "Menu."

In a city full of beautifully designed coffee shops competing on beans, latte art and interiors, Breka chose a different contest entirely. That choice is the whole story.

The usual disclosure: **Breka is not a Kuipra client.** This is an outside teardown built from Breka's own website and local news coverage. The company has not commented publicly on its strategy, so where we read between the lines, we say so.

The facts. Breka was, in its own words, "born in 2006 at Fraser and 49th, in the lively neighbourhood of Sunset," when it took over a well-established German bakery; some of the original recipes, such as Bienenstich, are still made. It is family-owned. By 2016 it had three locations, on Bute, Davie and Fraser. Kitsilano followed in 2017, then Denman Street in 2018 in a former Dairy Queen, Main Street around 2021 and Hastings-Sunrise in 2024. In June 2026 a Surrey location opened at the King George Hub, which the [Surrey Now-Leader](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/06/18/24-hour-breka-bakery-opens-in-surrey-at-food-focused-king-george-hub/) called the ninth in the chain. 2026 is its twentieth year.

## Compete on availability, not taste

Most food businesses compete on the product: better coffee, better croissants, better room. In Vancouver that is a crowded race, and every new specialty café raises the bar.

Breka competes on something almost nobody else offers: being open. That single operational decision creates a set of customers its competitors cannot reach at all:

- **Students.** Daily Hive's coverage describes the appeal for anyone "cramming for midterms" or "a night owl who wants a cozy drink and pastry at 2 am." UBC's student paper, the Ubyssey, has called it "the perfect study spot."
- **The end-of-night crowd.** One customer at the Surrey opening described it to the Now-Leader as "a go-to spot, basically, at the end of a night."
- **People who work odd hours.** Breka's About page mentions catering for film crews in "Hollywood North," an industry that rarely keeps nine-to-five hours.

None of these customers chose Breka over a competitor. At 2 a.m., there usually is no competitor. That is what makes it such strong positioning: it does not need to win a comparison, because it removes the comparison.

## A promise only works if you keep it every time

Positioning on availability has one rule: you cannot break it. A café that is closed when you expected it to be open has disappointed you. A 24-hour café that is closed when you arrive has broken the only thing you knew about it.

Breka's behaviour suggests it understands this. In late October 2026, when it moved to a new online ordering platform, it published an [important notice](https://www.breka.ca/important-notice) saying each store would close for "approximately 2 hours overnight" during the switch and then resume its regular 24-hour operations. Even a back-office systems migration was planned around the promise rather than the other way around.

For any small business that leans on hours, whether that is late closing, early opening or weekend service, the practical version is simple: keep your hours accurate everywhere customers check them, especially around holidays. ## Cheap enough to become a habit

Breka's prices are modest for Vancouver. Its [selection page](https://www.breka.ca/our-selection) lists a glazed donut at $1.75, cookies at $1.50 and an all-butter croissant at $3.50.

This is our reading rather than a stated strategy, but the price points fit the positioning. A late-night visit only becomes a habit if it is an easy yes. A student who comes in three nights a week during exams needs it to be affordable; a $9 pastry would turn a routine into an occasional treat.

## Word of mouth first, then the megaphone

Breka's About page says its "reputation has always been based on word-of-mouth." The website leans into that: the homepage carries quotes from Google reviews and an Instagram feed.

New locations are announced on Instagram, and local outlets such as Daily Hive have covered openings one after another, from [Denman in 2018](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/breka-bakery-denman-open-24-7) to [Hastings-Sunrise in 2024](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/breka-east-hastings-sunrise). A clear, unusual promise is easy to write about. "A bakery that never closes is opening near you" is a story; "a nice bakery is opening" is not.

## Heritage kept quietly

Breka did not start from scratch. It took over an established German bakery and kept some of its recipes, like Bienenstich. It does not make a big marketing story of this, but it is a useful detail for anyone buying an existing business: the regulars came for something, and keeping the things they recognize buys you time while you build your own promise on top.

## Slow growth, then infrastructure

From three locations in 2016 to nine in 2026 is steady, not explosive. One reason may be that 24-hour service is expensive to replicate: every new store needs overnight staffing and a steady supply of fresh product around the clock.

In July 2026, [Daily Hive reported](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/8555-cambie-street-vancouver-hotel-tower-breka-bakery-cafe) a proposal from a Breka-related entity for a 30-storey hotel at 8555 Cambie Street, built over more than 22,000 square feet of industrial bakery space. According to the report, the developer said the existing head bakery could become undersized given how quickly the chain is expanding. Whatever happens with the hotel, the bakery part points to the real constraint on a 24/7 brand: the promise at the counter depends on production capacity behind it.

## Three moves you can steal at any size

**Find the hours, days or occasions nobody serves.** Early-morning tradespeople, Sunday emergencies, late-night students, lunch for night-shift nurses. Being the only option at the right moment beats being the fifth-best option at a busy one.

**Protect the promise in your operations.** Staffing, holidays, system changes and supply all have to be planned around it. One locked door undoes years of trust.

**Make the habit an easy yes.** If your positioning depends on repeat visits, price the everyday item so that coming back needs no thought.

Breka did not become a Vancouver fixture by being the most beautiful café in the city. It became one by being open, every time.
