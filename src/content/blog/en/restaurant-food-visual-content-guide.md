Restaurants are the only businesses whose product is photographed by customers thousands of times a year for free — and most still let a decade-old, orange-lit photo represent them on Google Maps.

We covered the [full restaurant marketing system](/blog/restaurant-marketing-guide-vancouver/) separately. This guide goes deep on the single highest-leverage piece: the visual content itself — what to shoot, how, and where each image works for you.

## Where your photos actually sell (ranked)

1. **Google Business Profile.** The most-seen images you own. Diners scroll GBP photos like a menu before deciding — keep 30+ current, appetizing, real shots; refresh monthly; cover dishes, interior, exterior (people check for the vibe *and* the door).
2. **Delivery apps.** Items with photos outsell items without, on the same menu. If you're paying 15–30% commission anyway, don't hand the platform weak images.
3. **Your own menu and website.** Where photo quality justifies price. One caveat: only publish photos the kitchen can actually match — the gap between photo and plate is where trust dies.
4. **Social feeds.** The discovery layer — and where video, not stills, does the heavy lifting.

## Phone technique: the 80% that's free

- **Window light, always.** Daylight from the side beats every overhead bulb; restaurant lighting is designed for mood, not cameras. Shoot near the window at lunch, or invest $40 in a portable light for evening service.
- **Close and closer.** Fill the frame. Texture, steam, glaze and sauce sell; empty table around a small plate doesn't.
- **Match angle to dish.** Overhead (90°) for bowls, spreads and hot pot; 45° for burgers, stacks and anything with height; straight-on for cross-sections and layers.
- **Style in five seconds, not thirty.** Wipe the rim, add the garnish, angle the chopsticks — then shoot before the steam is gone. Speed beats fuss; food looks best in its first minute.

## Video: the moment beats the dish

The formats that reach non-followers are moments of motion: the pour, the pull, the sizzle, the reveal, the first cut. Fifteen seconds, phone-shot, captions on. This is the restaurant application of the [short-form video playbook](/blog/short-form-video-guide-vancouver/) — with one addition: **name the dish on screen.** A named signature ("the Volcano Beef Noodle") is what people search, save and order by name — the [named-product mechanic](/blog/aritzia-social-strategy-teardown/) at menu scale.

For Chinese-speaking diners, the same clips become Xiaohongshu video notes — where [detailed, honest food close-ups are the platform's native currency](/blog/xiaohongshu-restaurant-marketing-vancouver/) and local competition is still thin.

## Build the capture habit (or nothing else matters)

The difference between restaurants with endless content and restaurants with none isn't equipment — it's a system:

- **A standing shot list**: every new dish, every special, one interior refresh per season, one team moment per month.
- **A designated corner** with good light where plates get a five-second photo stop before service.
- **Customer content, recycled.** Repost tagged photos (with credit), and make the plate + table + lighting worth tagging — your customers are a volunteer camera crew if the food photographs well from a seat.
