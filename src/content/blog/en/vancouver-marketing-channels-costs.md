The most common question we get from Vancouver business owners isn't "which channel is best?" It's "what does this actually cost?" — and it's surprisingly hard to find a straight answer that isn't a sales pitch.

So here's our honest map of the main channels, with the price ranges we typically see and — more usefully — what moves the number up or down. Two caveats up front: ranges are for planning, not quotes (your industry, competition and goals change everything), and every figure here is a 2026 snapshot that will drift.

## Before the channels: the three-question filter

Any channel decision starts with three questions, and they matter more than any price list:

1. **What's the goal?** Leads this month, or a brand people recognize next year? Those point at different channels.
2. **How much time do you have?** Fast channels (ads) rent results; slow channels (SEO, content) build assets.
3. **What's the real budget?** Including your own hours, which are never free.

A channel that's "cheap" but doesn't fit your goal is the most expensive thing you can buy.

## Paid search (Google Ads)

You bid on searches and pay per click. In Vancouver we typically see local-service clicks in the **$2–8 range**, with competitive categories (legal, dental, real estate, home services) running **$10–30+**. A workable starting budget for one tight campaign is usually **$500–1,500/month** in ad spend, plus management (agencies typically charge $300–1,000+/month or a percentage of spend).

What moves the price: your industry's competition, how specific your keywords are, and landing-page quality — Google literally charges worse pages more per click. Paid search buys existing demand; it cannot create demand for something nobody searches.

## Social ads (Meta, TikTok)

Priced by impressions rather than clicks; you can start at **$10–20/day**, and a testing budget of **$300–1,000/month** is a realistic entry. Creative is the real cost driver — ads burn out, and the businesses that win are the ones feeding the machine fresh video and images, whether that's your time or a content retainer.

Social ads create demand rather than capture it — strongest for visual products, food, events and offers with broad appeal.

## Organic social and influencers

Organic posting costs no ad dollars and a lot of hours — realistically **5–10 hours/week** done properly, or roughly **$500–2,000+/month** outsourced depending on volume and platforms. Micro-influencers in Vancouver typically run **$100–500 per post** (nano creators sometimes work on product), rising steeply with audience size. We've covered [how to run influencer partnerships that don't flop](/blog/vancouver-influencer-marketing-guide/) separately.

Which platform deserves the hours is its own decision — our [Instagram vs TikTok vs Xiaohongshu comparison](/blog/instagram-tiktok-xiaohongshu-which-platform-vancouver/) breaks that down for Vancouver audiences, including when the Chinese-language platforms out-deliver the mainstream ones for a fraction of the ad cost.

## Local SEO and content

Your Google Business Profile is free; the cost is discipline — photos, posts, review responses, accurate hours. Systematic review collection is the highest-ROI activity most local businesses skip. Full local SEO (site optimization, content, citations) typically runs **$750–3,000/month** with an agency, or your own consistent hours with our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide-vancouver-small-business/).

This is the slow channel: months to move, but the results are an asset you own — unlike ads, rankings don't vanish the day you stop paying.

## Email and SMS

The unglamorous champion. Software runs **$20–100/month** for most small lists; the content can be an hour a week. Sent to people who already know you, email routinely produces the best revenue-per-dollar of any channel — the catch is you need the list first, which is why [we tell every local business to start building one now](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/).

## Local media and out-of-home

Sponsored articles and newsletter placements in Vancouver outlets typically run **$500–4,000 per campaign**; transit and outdoor start around **$1,000–3,000/month** for meaningful placements. These buy broad local awareness, not trackable leads — best when you have a location to fill or a launch to announce, and a brand worth remembering.

For businesses serving the Chinese community, the local Chinese-language media and platform ecosystem has its own price logic and its own outsized bargains — we cover it in our [guide to marketing to Vancouver's Chinese community](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/).

## How to choose on a small budget

Our default advice for a Vancouver business with under ~$1,500/month total:

1. **Fix the free foundation first** — Google Business Profile, reviews, a website that converts.
2. **Pick ONE paid or content channel** that matches your goal and audience. Concentration beats coverage; a channel needs enough budget and repetition to generate learnable data.
3. **Capture every contact into email** from day one, so each channel's spend leaves a permanent asset behind.
4. **Review at 90 days** with real numbers — cost per lead, not likes.

The businesses that waste the most money aren't the ones choosing the "wrong" channel — they're the ones running six channels at starvation budgets, learning nothing from any of them.
