The most common question we get from Vancouver business owners isn't "which channel is best?" It's "what does this actually cost?" — and it's surprisingly hard to find a straight answer that isn't a sales pitch.

So here's our honest map of the main channels, with the price ranges we typically see and — more usefully — what moves the number up or down. Two caveats up front: ranges are for planning, not quotes (your industry, competition and goals change everything), and every figure here is a 2026 snapshot that will drift.

**How to read the numbers (updated November 2026):** where we name a source, the figure is a published benchmark, usually from a vendor and often based on US data in US dollars, and we give the year. Everything else is our own planning estimate for Vancouver small businesses, in Canadian dollars. Neither is a promise of results.

## Before the channels: the three-question filter

Any channel decision starts with three questions, and they matter more than any price list:

1. **What's the goal?** Leads this month, or a brand people recognize next year? Those point at different channels.
2. **How much time do you have?** Fast channels (ads) rent results; slow channels (SEO, content) build assets.
3. **What's the real budget?** Including your own hours, which are never free.

A channel that's "cheap" but doesn't fit your goal is the most expensive thing you can buy.

## Paid search (Google Ads)

You bid on searches and pay per click. For a published benchmark, US ad-tech company LocaliQ's [2026 search advertising benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/), drawn from its own clients' campaigns, put the all-industry average at $5.42 per click, with attorneys at $9.87, home improvement at $8.33, dentists at $8.00 and restaurants at $2.05. Our planning estimate for Vancouver local-service clicks is **$2–8**, with the most contested categories (legal, dental, home services) running **$10–30+** on their best keywords. Before you budget, Google's [Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) will show estimated costs for your own keywords.

A workable starting budget for one tight campaign is usually **$500–1,500/month** in ad spend, plus management; in our experience agencies charge $300–1,000+/month or a percentage of spend.

What moves the price: your industry's competition, how specific your keywords are, and landing-page quality. Google says [higher-quality ads "can often lead to lower CPCs"](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722122), and landing-page experience is part of how it judges quality, so a weak page costs you twice. Our [landing page and conversion tracking guide](/blog/landing-page-conversion-tracking-guide/) covers the fixes. Paid search buys existing demand; it cannot create demand for something nobody searches.

One change since late 2025: in Canada, English-language Search campaigns are [automatically eligible to show ads inside AI Overviews and AI Mode](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775). There's no separate budget and no way to opt out of those placements on their own. Whether to keep renting that visibility or build it organically is the core question in [Google Ads vs. SEO](/blog/google-ads-vs-seo-small-business/).

## Social ads (Meta, TikTok)

Priced mostly by impressions rather than clicks. On Meta our usual starting point for a test is **$10–20/day**. TikTok sets a floor: its help centre says an ad group's daily budget [must exceed $20](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/budget?lang=en) (and a campaign-level daily budget must exceed $50). Across either platform, a testing budget of **$300–1,000/month** is a realistic entry in our estimate.

Creative is the real cost driver. Ads burn out, and the businesses that win are the ones feeding the machine fresh video and images, whether that's your time or a content retainer. Our [no-studio short-video playbook](/blog/short-form-video-guide-vancouver/) is how most small teams keep that cost manageable.

Social ads create demand rather than capture it — strongest for visual products, food, events and offers with broad appeal.

## Organic social and influencers

Organic posting costs no ad dollars and a lot of hours: in our experience **5–10 hours/week** done properly, or roughly **$500–2,000+/month** outsourced depending on volume and platforms. Our planning estimate for Vancouver micro-influencers is **$100–500 per post** (nano creators sometimes work on product), rising steeply with audience size. For a published comparison, Influencer Marketing Hub's [2026 micro-influencer rate guide](https://influencermarketinghub.com/micro-influencer-rates/) (a vendor benchmark, not Vancouver-specific) puts creators with 10,000–100,000 followers at about $150–500 per Instagram feed post and $200–800 per TikTok video. We've covered [how to run influencer partnerships that don't flop](/blog/vancouver-influencer-marketing-guide/) separately.

Which platform deserves the hours is its own decision — our [Instagram vs TikTok vs Xiaohongshu comparison](/blog/instagram-tiktok-xiaohongshu-which-platform-vancouver/) breaks that down for Vancouver audiences, including when the Chinese-language platforms out-deliver the mainstream ones for a fraction of the ad cost. If Xiaohongshu is the answer, our guide to [running it yourself or hiring help](/blog/xiaohongshu-marketing-vancouver/) covers what that costs.

## Local SEO and content

Your Google Business Profile is free; the cost is discipline — photos, posts, review responses, accurate hours. Systematic review collection is the highest-ROI activity most local businesses skip. Full local SEO (site optimization, content, citations) runs **$750–3,000/month** with an agency in our estimate. For a benchmark, Ahrefs' [survey of 439 SEO providers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/) (last updated August 2024, no currency stated) found local SEO averaged $1,557/month, with agencies averaging $1,819. Or put in your own consistent hours with our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide-vancouver-small-business/).

This is the slow channel: months to move, but the results are an asset you own — unlike ads, rankings don't vanish the day you stop paying. The same groundwork now feeds AI answers too. Google's own guidance says [optimizing for its generative AI search is still SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), so in our view you don't need a separate "AI visibility" budget to get started; our [GEO guide](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-guide/) covers what actually helps.

## Email and SMS

The unglamorous champion. Software runs **$20–100/month** for most small lists, and some tools are free for very small ones: Klaviyo's [pricing page](https://www.klaviyo.com/pricing), checked November 2026, is free up to 250 profiles and starts at US$20/month at 500. The content can be an hour a week. Sent to people who already know you, email is in our experience one of the best revenue-per-dollar channels a local business has. The catch is you need the list first, which is why [we tell every local business to start building one now](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/).

## Local media and out-of-home

Our planning estimates: sponsored articles and newsletter placements in Vancouver outlets typically run **$500–4,000 per campaign**, and transit and outdoor start around **$1,000–3,000/month** for meaningful placements. Rate cards here are rarely public, so get current quotes before planning around these. These channels buy broad local awareness, not trackable leads — best when you have a location to fill or a launch to announce, and a brand worth remembering.

For businesses serving the Chinese community, the local Chinese-language media and platform ecosystem has its own price logic and its own outsized bargains — we cover it in our [guide to marketing to Vancouver's Chinese community](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/).

## How to choose on a small budget

Our default advice for a Vancouver business with under ~$1,500/month total:

1. **Fix the free foundation first** — Google Business Profile, reviews, a website that converts. (Don't wait for a grant to cover the website; as of late 2026 [none of the old ones are open](/blog/canada-small-business-marketing-grants/).)
2. **Pick ONE paid or content channel** that matches your goal and audience. Concentration beats coverage; a channel needs enough budget and repetition to generate learnable data.
3. **Capture every contact into email** from day one, so each channel's spend leaves a permanent asset behind.
4. **Review at 90 days** with real numbers — cost per lead, not likes.

The businesses that waste the most money aren't the ones choosing the "wrong" channel — they're the ones running six channels at starvation budgets, learning nothing from any of them.
