Clinic marketing has a unique constraint no restaurant or salon deals with: you're marketing a regulated health service, under a college that has opinions about your ads — and under patients' most trust-sensitive purchase decision. Get it right and marketing compounds for years. Get it wrong and you're explaining a complaint to your college.

This guide covers the playbook for Vancouver dental, TCM, physio, chiro and aesthetics practices — including where the lines are.

## First, know your lines

Every regulated health profession in BC answers to a college with advertising standards, and they are not identical. Recurring themes across professions:

- **Patient testimonials are restricted or prohibited** for several colleges — the review culture that powers restaurants doesn't transfer cleanly.
- **Superlatives and comparisons** ("Vancouver's best," "#1 clinic") are generally off-limits.
- **Outcome promises and before/after imagery** face profession-specific rules, especially in aesthetics.
- **Titles and credentials** must be accurate and non-misleading.

None of this prevents effective marketing — it *shapes* it toward education and verifiable fact, which happens to be what patients trust anyway. Check your college's current standards before campaigning; if a marketing vendor promises tactics that ignore them, that's your cue to leave. (It's one of the filter questions in our [choosing an agency guide](/blog/how-to-choose-marketing-agency-vancouver/).)

## The channel that matters: search and Maps

Patients pick clinics like this: search "dentist near me" or a symptom, scan the map pack, compare reviews and distance, check insurance and direct billing, book. Every step happens before you know they exist. So:

- **Google Business Profile, complete and precise** — exact category, services listed, insurance/direct-billing noted, booking link live, real photos of the space (patients are checking whether it looks clean and modern). Full setup in our [GBP guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/).
- **Reviews, handled conservatively** — easy to leave, never incentivized, every response privacy-safe ("Thank you — we're glad to hear this" beats anything that confirms someone was a patient).
- **A website that answers the deciding questions** on the homepage: services, languages spoken, direct billing, parking, how to book. If patients call to ask it, it belongs on the page — that's the core of our [12-point conversion audit](/blog/website-conversion-audit-checklist/).

## Education content: the compliant growth engine

The highest-ROI clinic content is answering real patient questions in plain language: "Does a deep cleaning hurt?" "What does cupping actually do?" "How many physio sessions does a sprain need?" This works triple duty — it ranks for the searches patients actually make, it pre-builds trust before the first visit, and it's the content format *every* college is comfortable with: educational, factual, no promises.

One well-written answer per week beats an expensive campaign per quarter. And in the AI era it compounds further — clear Q&A content is exactly what [AI assistants cite when recommending local providers](/blog/how-ai-assistants-recommend-local-businesses/).

## The bilingual trust factor

For Vancouver clinics, language is a clinical-trust feature, not a marketing nicety. "國語/粵語服務" on your profile and site is a deciding factor for a large share of Richmond and Vancouver patients — and for TCM especially, Chinese-language content on WeChat and Xiaohongshu reaches patients researching in their own language, where health decisions actually get discussed. The same rule applies as everywhere: [translated captions aren't localized presence](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/) — the clinic that answers questions natively in Chinese wins the referral group chat.

## Retention: recall is marketing too

A dental practice's revenue lives in hygiene recall; physio in treatment plans; aesthetics in maintenance cycles. Automated, personal-feeling recall messages (due-date reminders, seasonal checkup pushes, direct-billing renewal notes) cost almost nothing and out-earn any acquisition campaign. The [email principles](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/) apply — with the added rule that health communication must stay privacy-safe: no diagnosis details in a subject line, ever.

## What we'd skip

Aggressive discounting (it attracts price-shoppers and sits badly with professional standards). Buying followers or engagement anywhere. Superlative-heavy ads a college could read as comparative claims. And any vendor who doesn't ask which college regulates you before proposing tactics.
