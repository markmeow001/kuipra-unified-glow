Short-form video is the highest-reach organic format on every platform that matters — and the most intimidating for small business owners, who picture editing suites and camera crews. The truth is friendlier: the algorithm actively favours the un-produced. What it punishes is inconsistency and boring first seconds.

Here's the playbook we hand Vancouver businesses that have a phone, a ring light budget of $40, and no film degree.

## Why this format is worth your limited hours

Feed posts mostly reach people who already follow you. Short video is the discovery format: Reels, TikTok and Shorts are shown primarily to *non-followers*, judged by watch-time, not by your follower count. For a small account, that's the whole game — it's the only organic format where a 200-follower business can reach 20,000 local viewers because one video earned it.

## The hook is 80% of the job

Viewers decide in under two seconds. Not clever — clear:

- **Say the payoff first.** "Three things your strata doesn't tell you" beats a logo animation every time.
- **Show the end at the start.** The finished cake, the transformed room, the after — then rewind to process.
- **Text hook on frame one**, because most viewers start muted. Captions throughout, always.

If you steal one habit: write the first line *before* filming anything.

## Five formats a two-person business can sustain

1. **Process** — the thing you already do, sped up: prep, install, plating, pour. Zero script, endlessly repeatable.
2. **Answer one question** — the questions customers ask weekly, one per video, straight to camera. (Your FAQ page is a content calendar in disguise.)
3. **Before / after** — the native format of trades, beauty, detailing, design. The reveal IS the hook.
4. **Local list or take** — "three underrated lunch spots on this block," "what $2,000 of bathroom reno actually buys." Local content earns local followers, which is the point.
5. **The named signature** — one product or service with a name and a moment, filmed often. The [Aritzia Super Puff logic](/blog/aritzia-social-strategy-teardown/): speakable things get shared.

Batch-film a month of these in one afternoon. Consistency is a production decision, not a willpower one — the same principle as your [content calendar](/blog/content-calendar-for-small-business/).

## Film once, post four places

Vertical 9:16 travels: Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Xiaohongshu video notes all accept the same file. Two Vancouver-specific notes:

- **Xiaohongshu is the underpriced one.** Almost no local businesses post video there, while Chinese-speaking customers search it daily — the arbitrage our [platform comparison](/blog/instagram-tiktok-xiaohongshu-which-platform-vancouver/) covers in depth.
- **Don't crosspost watermarks.** TikTok-watermarked video gets suppressed on Reels and vice versa; export clean and upload natively.

Where AI helps: captions, cutting silences, resizing, translating subtitles for bilingual posting. Where it doesn't: [being the face and voice of your business](/blog/ai-social-content-boundaries/) — viewers follow humans.

## Measure what matters

Ignore likes. Watch three numbers: **watch-through** (are the first seconds working?), **saves and shares** (did it earn attention worth keeping?), and **profile visits → website taps** (is reach becoming business?). One video a month that drives ten inquiries beats a viral hit that drives none — reach is the means, [conversion is the score](/blog/website-conversion-audit-checklist/).
