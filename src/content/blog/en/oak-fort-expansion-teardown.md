If you have walked through Gastown, you have probably seen Oak + Fort: white walls, pale wood, clothes in cream, grey and black, arranged like a gallery. The look has barely changed in more than a decade, and it is one of the more recognizable retail aesthetics to come out of Vancouver.

In June 2025, the company behind it sought creditor protection.

Both of those things are true, and the gap between them is the useful part. A brand can be clear, consistent and well liked, and still get into serious trouble, because brand and business model are not the same thing.

The usual disclosure: **Oak + Fort is not a Kuipra client.** This is an outside teardown. Unusually for a private company, much of its financial story is public, because it is in court filings from its 2025 restructuring. We rely on those documents and on press coverage, and we have tried to report them fairly. The company is still operating.

## The brand that worked

The facts first. Oak + Fort was established in 2010. It began, according to an early [Daily Hive interview](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/vancouver-boutiques-oak-fort), as an online store based in Edmonton, and opened its first physical store in Gastown in December 2010. Court documents name Min Kang as the founder, and the brand's [own site](https://www.oakandfort.com/pages/about-us) describes it as a woman-founded and woman-led business offering design at an "approachable price."

The aesthetic did the marketing. When Oak + Fort opened at Westfield Century City in Los Angeles in 2017, [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lifestyle/style/oak-fort-opens-at-westfield-century-city-1001333/) compared it to Everlane for its "clean aesthetic and crisp visuals," and described white walls and wooden panels. [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/retail/oak-fort-opens-first-la-flagship/2017041815494) put prices at roughly $28 to $208. The 5,000-square-foot Gastown flagship that opened in 2022 carried womenswear, menswear, accessories and homeware, with space for guest brands, according to [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/oak-fort-gastown-flagship).

That consistency is a real achievement. A store from 2017 and a store today read as the same brand. We made a similar point about [Aritzia's quiet consistency](/blog/aritzia-social-strategy-teardown/), and our [visual identity guide](/blog/visual-identity-basics-small-business/) covers which elements are worth locking down. Oak + Fort is a good example of the first half of that lesson. The rest of this story is about what consistency cannot do on its own.

## The expansion

For roughly a decade, Oak + Fort grew without outside investors. The founder's [affidavit](https://cdncases.alixpartners.com/docs/default-source/insolvency-case-documents/oak-and-fort/ccaa-proceedings/motion-materials/affidavit-1-of-min-gyoung-kang-made-june-6-2025.pdf) says the company grew "without external funding."

Then it sped up. In March 2022, [Retail Insider](https://retail-insider.com/retail-insider/2022/03/canadian-fashion-retailer-oak-fort-to-open-multiple-store-locations-in-2022-including-gastown-flagship-interview/) reported 27 stores, 20 in Canada and seven in the U.S. By June 2025, the affidavit counted 42: 26 in Canada and 16 in the U.S. Between 2021 and 2024 alone, it opened 14 new stores in Canada and 12 in the U.S.

Here is the number that matters. According to the financial statements attached to the affidavit, revenue was $94.2 million in fiscal 2023, $93.9 million in fiscal 2024 and $93.8 million in fiscal 2025 (the latter two unaudited and in draft respectively). Over the same three years, the company reported net losses of about $1.1 million, $10.7 million and $5.1 million.

More stores, the same revenue, continued losses. Each new location brought rent, staff and inventory, but the total business did not get bigger. This is our reading rather than the company's explanation, but the pattern suggests new stores were often spreading existing demand across more locations rather than finding new customers.

## The channel that was under-funded

By 2025, stores produced about 85% of revenue and e-commerce about 15%, according to the affidavit. The same document says the expansion years came with "an under-investment" in the e-commerce platforms.

That is a striking admission for a brand that started online. A business that began as a web store ended up with most of its money tied up in leases, and its own digital channel falling behind. ## The pressure that arrived last

The affidavit also points to tariffs. About 68% of the company's products were made in China, and tariffs, it says, "heavily eroded margins." A business already stretched by expansion had little room to absorb a cost shock.

## The restructuring

Oak + Fort filed notices of intention in early June 2025 and obtained an initial order under Canada's Companies' Creditors Arrangement Act on June 6, as [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-oak-and-fort-granted-creditor-protection/) reported. The affidavit put its debts at more than $25 million.

What followed is documented in the court-appointed monitor's reports. The company chose to exit two locations in Canada and six in the U.S. Creditors voted on a restructuring plan, and according to the monitor's [sixth report](https://cdncases.alixpartners.com/docs/default-source/insolvency-case-documents/oak-and-fort/ccaa-proceedings/reports/2025-12-23-sixth-report-of-the-monitor.pdf), all 27 creditors who voted approved it. The monitor's [fifth report](https://cdncases.alixpartners.com/docs/default-source/insolvency-case-documents/oak-and-fort/ccaa-proceedings/reports/fifth-report-of-the-monitor-dated-november-26-2025.pdf) estimated unsecured creditors would receive 4% under the plan, compared with an estimated recovery of less than 1% to 2% in a liquidation. About $4 million of new equity came in, the interim lenders included the founder, and an exit credit facility of at least $7 million was arranged. The plan was implemented on January 28, 2026.

Creditors, in other words, judged the business more valuable alive than broken up. The brand's own site now describes "30+" locations under the name OAK Studio.

## Three moves you can steal at any size

**Make each new location prove itself before the next one.** Set a target for every new store, chair or service area, and wait until it is hit. If total revenue is not growing as locations are added, stop opening and find out why.

**Do not starve the channel you own.** A website does not carry a lease. Keep investing in it even when physical expansion is exciting, and especially if your business started online.

**Know your exposure before it becomes a crisis.** If most of your product comes from one country, one supplier or one landlord, plan now for what happens if that cost jumps.

Oak + Fort's look was never the problem. The lesson is that a strong brand buys you attention, not unlimited room to expand.
