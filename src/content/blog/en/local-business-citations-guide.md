Here is an uncomfortable exercise. Search your business name plus your city, then look past the first three results. Somewhere down that page — or on page two — there is probably a listing you did not create, with a phone number you stopped using, or a suite number from a move you made years ago.

That listing is still answering questions about you.

Most local businesses treat their online presence as a website plus a Google listing. In practice you exist across a network of databases that were populated by scrapers, aggregators and well-meaning strangers, and **none of them sync with each other**. This is the least glamorous work in local marketing and one of the highest-return.

## Why this matters more now than it did three years ago

The old argument for citations was trust signals: when many independent sources agree on your name, address and phone number, search engines treat those details as reliable. That still holds, and it still affects map rankings.

The new argument is bigger. **AI assistants answer local questions using sources you may never have looked at.**

When someone asks an assistant for the best clinic or restaurant or contractor in their area, the answer is assembled from whatever the assistant can reach and trust: map data, directory pages, review platforms, and "best of" listicles. Assistants that browse the live web have leaned on Bing's index rather than Google's — which produces the strange situation where a business can rank respectably on Google and still be **completely invisible** to an assistant, because it was never in the other database.

We covered the decision mechanics in [how AI assistants decide which businesses to recommend](/blog/how-ai-assistants-recommend-local-businesses/). This article is the plumbing underneath it.

## The tiers, in priority order

Not all listings are worth your time. Work down this list and stop when the returns flatten.

**Tier 1 — the map layers.** Google Business Profile first; it remains the single highest-value listing and drives the map pack. Our [full GBP setup guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/) covers it properly. Then **Bing Places**, which is free, takes about twenty minutes, and often imports directly from your Google listing — this is the one most Canadian businesses skip, and it is the one that feeds assistants. Then **Apple Business Connect**, because every iPhone user asking Maps for directions is querying Apple's data, not Google's.

**Tier 2 — the platforms that get cited.** Yelp still carries weight in aggregated answers well beyond its own traffic. Add the review and booking platforms specific to your industry — the ones your customers already use to compare providers.

**Tier 3 — legitimate directories and associations.** Your chamber of commerce, your professional association, your BIA, industry bodies, and the agency-comparison directories if you are in a service category. These matter because they are exactly the pages that get pulled into "best X in Vancouver" answers — a pattern we saw directly in our own visibility testing, where directory pages outranked every individual business for several commercial queries.

**Tier 0 — what to skip.** Pay-to-list directories with no editorial standard, link-farm sites, and anything selling "500 citations for $99." They add nothing, and cleaning them up later costs more than never having them.

## Consistency beats completeness

One rule governs all of this: **the details must match, character for character, everywhere.**

Not "close enough." Suite 210 – 1055 West Broadway and 1055 W Broadway #210 are the same place to a human and two different strings to a database. Pick one canonical format for your name, address and phone number, write it down somewhere your whole team can see, and use exactly that everywhere.

Three specifics that bite Vancouver businesses in particular:

- **The legal name versus the trading name.** Pick the one customers say out loud and use it consistently. Do not append keywords to it — "Kuipra Communications | Vancouver Marketing Agency SEO" in a listing name is a policy violation on Google, and it can get the profile suspended.
- **Bilingual names.** If you operate in English and Chinese, decide which name is the listing name and keep the other in the description. Two different primary names across platforms reads as two different businesses.
- **Phone numbers.** A tracking number in a listing that differs from the number on your site is the most common consistency break we see. If you must use call tracking, make the tracking number the one displayed everywhere, not a second number.

## Auditing what is already out there

Run this once, then annually.

1. **Search your business name plus your city**, and go three pages deep. Note every listing you find, including the ones you did not create.
2. **Search your old phone number and your old address** if you have ever moved or changed numbers. This is where the ghosts live.
3. **For each listing:** claim it if you can, correct it, or request removal if it is a duplicate.
4. **Check what the assistants say.** Ask two or three AI assistants a question a customer would actually ask in your category, and see whether you appear and whether the details are right. Repeat the same query a few times — answers vary between runs, so a single miss is not proof of invisibility.
5. **Write down what you found**, so next year's audit is a diff instead of a fresh archaeology dig.

Step 4 is the one almost nobody does, and it is now the one that tells you the most.

## What this does and does not fix

Citations are a floor, not a ceiling. Getting them right removes a reason for search engines and assistants to distrust or ignore you. It does not, by itself, make you the recommended choice — that still comes from reviews, from content that answers real questions, and from being genuinely good at the thing.

Think of it as the plumbing. Nobody compliments you for having it. Everybody notices when it leaks.

If your listings are already clean, the next constraint is usually the site itself — whether it answers the deciding questions fast enough to convert the traffic you are earning. That is the [twelve-point conversion audit](/blog/website-conversion-audit-checklist/).
