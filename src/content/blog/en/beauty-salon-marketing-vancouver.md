A beauty business has one of the easiest marketing stories to tell — the work is visual, the results are immediate, and happy clients literally wear your portfolio out the door. Yet most Vancouver salons market like it's 2015: a dusty Instagram, a half-filled Google profile, and a prayer.

Here's the playbook we'd hand any salon, nail studio, lash artist or barbershop in Metro Vancouver.

## Win the "near me" moment first

Beauty searches are overwhelmingly local and time-pressed: "nails near me," "balayage Richmond," "mens fade Burnaby." That's a Google Maps battle, and it's won on profile quality, not luck:

- **Category precision matters** — "Nail salon," "Hair salon," "Barber shop" are different rankings. Set the exact primary category, add the secondaries you genuinely serve.
- **Photos are the menu.** Load real work (not stock), interior shots, and refresh monthly. Beauty is one of the few industries where customers *scroll GBP photos like a portfolio* before booking.
- **Reviews with service names in them.** A review that says "best gel-X in Richmond" is ranking fuel for that exact search. Ask happy clients at checkout, QR on the front desk. The full system is in our [reviews guide](/blog/google-reviews-management-guide/).
- **Booking link wired in** so a searcher can go from Maps to a booked slot in two taps.

## Think in neighbourhoods, not "Vancouver"

Metro Vancouver beauty clients rarely search city-wide — they search where they live and work: "lash extensions Kitsilano," "nail salon Metrotown," "balayage Mount Pleasant." A site that only ever says "Vancouver" is invisible to most of those searches:

- **Name your neighbourhood everywhere it counts** — in your GBP description, your homepage title ("— Nail Studio in Mount Pleasant"), and naturally inside service pages.
- **Anchor yourself to landmarks people actually use.** "Two minutes from Metrotown Station" helps clients find you and matches how locals phrase searches and reviews.
- **If you genuinely serve several areas, give each its own home** — a short neighbourhood section or page each beats cramming ten area names into one sentence, which reads as spam to both customers and Google.

## Before-and-after is your entire content strategy

You don't need content ideas; you produce content with every appointment. What you need is a *capture habit*:

- **Shoot the after, every time.** Same corner of the studio, same light (a $40 ring light makes every chair a studio), five seconds per client. Consent first — most clients are flattered.
- **Short video beats stills.** The transformation reveal, the gel cure, the curl drop — 15 seconds, no talking needed. This is the most under-used free reach in the industry.
- **Name your signature services.** "The Glass Skin Facial" gets asked for by name and searched by name; "Facial #3" doesn't. It's the same named-product mechanic we broke down in our [Aritzia teardown](/blog/aritzia-social-strategy-teardown/).

## The Xiaohongshu advantage (Vancouver-specific)

If any of your clientele is Chinese-speaking, Xiaohongshu is the highest-leverage channel you're probably not on. Beauty is one of XHS's biggest verticals; users search it like Google before choosing a salon, and Richmond/Vancouver beauty notes routinely outperform what the same effort earns on Instagram. Style notes, nail-art close-ups, honest process shots — authentic beats polished. Our [platform comparison](/blog/instagram-tiktok-xiaohongshu-which-platform-vancouver/) and [Chinese community marketing guide](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/) cover how to run it natively rather than reposting translated captions.

## Rebooking is the real revenue engine

New-client marketing is expensive; a beauty business lives on frequency. Nails on a 3-week cycle, hair on 8, lashes on 2-3 — which means your calendar's future is decided *at checkout*:

1. **Book the next visit before they leave.** "Want me to hold your spot three weeks out?" converts far better than any reminder text later. Tie your first-visit offer to it.
2. **Automate reminders** at 48 and 24 hours — most no-shows are memory failures, and reminder automation is cheap insurance on every slot.
3. **Build the list.** Phone numbers and emails, collected at booking, used for slow-day offers and product drops. [Email and SMS remain the channels you own](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/) when the algorithm changes.

## Pricing and promos without cheapening the brand

The discount trap hits beauty hard: Groupon-era salons trained a generation of deal-hoppers. Protect price integrity — modest first-visit incentive tied to rebooking, referral credit for regulars, and bundles (mani+pedi, cut+treatment) that raise ticket size. A full book at real prices beats a packed month of one-timers at 40% off.

## What to skip

Paid ads before the profile and portfolio are strong (you'd be paying to show people a weak storefront). Chasing TikTok trends unrelated to your craft. And six half-alive platforms — one great visual channel plus a bulletproof Google profile fills more chairs than everything at once.
