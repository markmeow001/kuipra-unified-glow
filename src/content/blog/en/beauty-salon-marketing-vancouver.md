A beauty business has one of the easiest marketing stories to tell — the work is visual, the results are immediate, and happy clients literally wear your portfolio out the door. Yet most Vancouver salons market like it's 2015: a dusty Instagram, a half-filled Google profile, and a prayer.

Here's the playbook we'd hand any salon, nail studio, lash artist or barbershop in Metro Vancouver.

## Win the "near me" moment first

Beauty searches are overwhelmingly local and time-pressed: "nails near me," "balayage Richmond," "mens fade Burnaby." That's a Google Maps battle, and it's won on profile quality, not luck:

- **Category precision matters** — "Nail salon," "Hair salon," "Barber shop" are different rankings. Set the exact primary category, add the secondaries you genuinely serve.
- **Photos are the menu.** Load real work (not stock), interior shots, and refresh monthly. In our experience, beauty clients scroll GBP photos like a portfolio before they book.
- **Reviews, asked for the same way every time.** Detailed reviews that mention a service can help you match searches like "gel-X Richmond," but you can't script them. Google's 2026 policy says businesses should not ask customers to include specific content, pressure them to review while they're on the premises, or ask only the clients they expect to be happy ([Google policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)). Put a QR code on the front desk, include the review link in the post-visit message every client gets, and leave the wording to them. Clear service names (more on that below) give clients the words without you asking. The full system is in our [reviews guide](/blog/google-reviews-management-guide/).
- **Booking link wired in** so a searcher can go from Maps to a booked slot in two taps.
- **The same details everywhere else.** Copy your exact name, address, hours and booking link to Bing Places and Apple Business Connect, then keep directory listings consistent. Our [Bing Places walkthrough](/blog/bing-places-setup-guide/) and [citations guide](/blog/local-business-citations-guide/) cover the order to do it in.

## Think in neighbourhoods, not "Vancouver"

Metro Vancouver beauty clients rarely search city-wide — they search where they live and work: "lash extensions Kitsilano," "nail salon Metrotown," "balayage Mount Pleasant." A site that only ever says "Vancouver" is invisible to most of those searches:

- **Name your neighbourhood everywhere it counts** — in your GBP description, your homepage title ("— Nail Studio in Mount Pleasant"), and naturally inside service pages.
- **But keep it out of your Google business name** unless it's on your sign. Google says unnecessary information in the name isn't permitted and could get your profile suspended ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)).
- **Anchor yourself to landmarks people actually use.** "Two minutes from Metrotown Station" helps clients find you and matches how locals phrase searches and reviews.
- **If you genuinely serve several areas, give each its own home** — a short neighbourhood section or page each beats cramming ten area names into one sentence, which reads as spam to both customers and Google. Our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide-vancouver-small-business/) goes deeper on neighbourhood pages.

## Before-and-after is your entire content strategy

You don't need content ideas; you produce content with every appointment. What you need is a *capture habit*:

- **Shoot the after, every time.** Same corner of the studio, same light (an inexpensive ring light makes every chair a studio), five seconds per client. Consent first, and ask separately before showing a face; in our experience many clients are flattered to be asked.
- **Short video beats stills.** The transformation reveal, the gel cure, the curl drop — 15 seconds, no talking needed. In our experience it's the most under-used free reach in the industry, and our [short-form video playbook](/blog/short-form-video-guide-vancouver/) covers the hooks and formats a small studio can keep up.
- **Name your signature services.** "The Glass Skin Facial" gets asked for by name and searched by name; "Facial #3" doesn't. It's the same named-product mechanic we broke down in our [Aritzia teardown](/blog/aritzia-social-strategy-teardown/).

## The Xiaohongshu advantage (Vancouver-specific)

If any of your clientele is Chinese-speaking, Xiaohongshu is the highest-leverage channel you're probably not on. Beauty is one of the categories the platform is best known for, and many users search it like Google before choosing a salon. In our experience, Richmond and Vancouver beauty notes often earn more from the same effort than Instagram posts do. Style notes, nail-art close-ups, honest process shots — authentic beats polished. Our [platform comparison](/blog/instagram-tiktok-xiaohongshu-which-platform-vancouver/) and [Chinese community marketing guide](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/) cover how to run it natively rather than reposting translated captions, and the [full Xiaohongshu guide](/blog/xiaohongshu-marketing-vancouver/) walks through running it yourself versus hiring an agency. Many Chinese-speaking regulars would also rather book and get reminders on WeChat; our [WeChat private traffic playbook](/blog/wechat-private-traffic-playbook/) covers setting that up on an account the business owns.

## Rebooking is the real revenue engine

New-client marketing is expensive; a beauty business lives on frequency. Nails come back on roughly a 3-week cycle, lashes every 2–3 weeks, hair around every 8 — which means your calendar's future is decided *at checkout*:

1. **Book the next visit before they leave.** "Want me to hold your spot three weeks out?" converts far better than any reminder text later. Tie your first-visit offer to it.
2. **Automate reminders** at 48 and 24 hours — many no-shows are simple memory failures, and reminder automation is cheap insurance on every slot.
3. **Build the list.** Phone numbers and emails, collected at booking, used for slow-day offers and product drops. [Email and SMS remain the channels you own](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/) when the algorithm changes. Under CASL, a client who bought from you in the last two years gives you implied consent to receive offers, and every message needs your business name and an unsubscribe ([CASL](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html)); an opt-in at booking is cleaner still.

## Pricing and promos without cheapening the brand

The discount trap hits beauty hard: in our reading, the Groupon era trained a generation of deal-hoppers. Protect price integrity — modest first-visit incentive tied to rebooking, referral credit for regulars, and bundles (mani+pedi, cut+treatment) that raise ticket size. A full book at real prices beats a packed month of one-timers at 40% off. One promo that's off the table entirely: anything tied to a review. Google bans discounts or freebies in exchange for reviews, even honest ones.

## What to skip

Paid ads before the profile and portfolio are strong (you'd be paying to show people a weak storefront; our [Vancouver channel cost guide](/blog/vancouver-marketing-channels-costs/) shows what that traffic costs). Chasing TikTok trends unrelated to your craft. And six half-alive platforms — one great visual channel plus a bulletproof Google profile fills more chairs than everything at once.
