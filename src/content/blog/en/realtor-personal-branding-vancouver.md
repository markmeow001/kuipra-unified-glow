[Greater Vancouver Realtors](https://storeys.com/rebgv-rebrand-greater-vancouver-realtors/), the board that covers Vancouver and much of the region around it, has more than fifteen thousand members. In January 2026, [12,628 homes were listed for sale](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/real-estate/2026/02/03/vancouver-home-sales-continue-sluggish-pace-to-kick-off-2026-real-estate-board/) on that board's MLS: fewer listings than agents. Every agent has access to the same MLS, the same market data, the same open-house playbook — which means the product being marketed isn't the house. It's you.

Here's how the agents who win referrals actually build that.

## Niche first, brand second

"I serve all of Metro Vancouver, all property types" is the realtor version of the positioning mistake every business makes — [being everything means being nothing](/blog/how-to-write-a-positioning-statement/). The agents who compound pick an ownable intersection:

- **A neighbourhood** (East Van character homes, Brentwood pre-sales, Steveston)
- **A life moment** (first-time buyers, growing families, downsizers, investors exiting)
- **A community** — language, culture, profession (nurses, tech workers, new immigrants)

The niche isn't a cage; it's a memory hook. "The Burnaby pre-sale guy" gets mentioned in group chats. "A realtor" doesn't.

## Content that proves expertise (not activity)

Most realtor content proves the agent exists — sold signs, key handoffs, market-update reposts. Referral-generating content proves the agent *knows things*:

- **Answer the questions your niche actually Googles**: strata document red flags, presale deposit structures, what the latest mortgage rule change means for a first buyer, school catchments, assignment tax basics.
- **Neighbourhood content only a local could make** — walkthroughs, "what $900K buys here vs there," the coffee-shop test. This is where short video shines: you, on location, talking like a human. Our [short-form video playbook](/blog/short-form-video-guide-vancouver/) covers the hooks and formats a very small team can actually keep up.
- **Document the market, don't just react to headlines.** A monthly "what I'm actually seeing in [niche]" note builds more authority than reposting national news — and it's exactly the kind of first-hand content [AI assistants cite](/blog/how-ai-assistants-recommend-local-businesses/) when someone asks for a local expert.

Consistency beats production value. One honest phone-shot video per week for a year outperforms a cinematic brand film nobody follows up.

## The Chinese-community channels (Vancouver's edge)

For a huge share of Vancouver's market, property decisions are researched and discussed in Chinese — on WeChat, in Xiaohongshu notes, in family group chats. If you serve this market (or want to), presence there isn't optional:

- **Xiaohongshu**: in our experience, neighbourhood guides, buying-process explainers and honest market notes often do better there than their Instagram equivalents. Search-driven, save-driven — evergreen guides keep working for months. Our [Xiaohongshu guide](/blog/xiaohongshu-marketing-vancouver/) walks through running it yourself versus hiring help.
- **WeChat**: where the referral actually happens. A useful official-account article gets forwarded into exactly the family group chat you can't buy your way into. [The ecosystem has its own logic](/blog/wechat-marketing-canadian-business/) — treat it natively, and once people add you, our [WeChat private traffic playbook](/blog/wechat-private-traffic-playbook/) covers tagging contacts and running groups people stay in.
- **Bilingual done properly** — not machine-translated captions. [Translated is not localized](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/), and in a trust business the difference is loud.

One compliance detail that catches people on these channels: BCFSA says [translating brokerage names into other languages is not acceptable](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/practice-resources/advertising-checklist-and-sample-advertising). Your Xiaohongshu and WeChat profiles need the brokerage name exactly as registered, even when everything else is in Chinese.

## The boring machine behind every "lucky" agent

Referrals feel like luck; they're built like infrastructure:

1. **A database, actually maintained.** Every past client, every open-house conversation, tagged by niche and timeline. Your future commissions live here, not on Instagram.
2. **A rhythm of useful touch** — a monthly email that says something real about your niche ([the channel you own](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/)), assessment-notice season notes, a call on their purchase anniversary. One Canadian catch: under [CASL](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html), implied consent from a business relationship covers two years after a purchase and six months after an inquiry. Your referral cycle runs longer than both, so ask for explicit consent to stay in touch.
3. **Google reviews as social proof** — collected the way Google's [review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) now requires: ask every client, not only the happy ones; offer nothing in return (no gift card tucked into the closing gift); don't ask for specific wording; and, since the February 2026 update, don't pressure people to review on the spot. Reviews from your team or family need to disclose the connection, per the [Competition Bureau](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2024/01/online-reviews-posted-by-employees-businesses-could-be-liable.html). The agent a stranger finds on Maps with 80 thoughtful reviews starts every conversation ahead. ([System, not luck.](/blog/google-reviews-management-guide/))
4. **A Business Profile under your real name.** Google [lets individual practitioners have their own profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) if they're public-facing and reachable at the verified location, but the name field is your name, not "Burnaby Presale Expert". Extra keywords in the name can get a profile suspended. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/) covers the setup.

## Stay inside the lines

BC realtor advertising answers to BCFSA, and its [advertising guidelines](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/guidelines/advertising-guidelines) and [advertising checklist](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/practice-resources/advertising-checklist-and-sample-advertising) are more specific than most agents remember:

- **Full brokerage name, prominently.** The full registered name, not a short form, and not in small type at the bottom of the page.
- **Social profiles are advertising.** The brokerage name (and your team name, if you're on a team) goes on your profile screen; individual posts don't each need it. On YouTube, it goes in each video's title or description.
- **Your licensee name.** Your legal name, a recognizable short form, or a name BCFSA approved. If you have a personal real estate corporation, ads use the corporation's licensee name.
- **Claims need their basis.** "Top producer" or "#1 in Richmond" must state the source, date and qualifying information.
- **Teams register first.** A team [can't advertise as a team](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/teams-information) until BCFSA approves its registration, and unlicensed assistants shown in ads must be identified as unlicensed.

None of it blocks a strong personal brand — it just rules out the shortcuts that look like a strong brand.
