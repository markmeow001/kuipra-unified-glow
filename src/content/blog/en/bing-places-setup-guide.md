We covered *why* Bing Places matters in the [citation map guide](/blog/local-business-citations-guide/): assistants that browse the live web have leaned on Bing's index, so a business missing from Bing can be invisible to them while still ranking on Google.

This is the part that guide skipped — actually doing it. It takes one sitting. The checking afterwards is what most people skip, and it is the part that decides whether the listing helps you.

## Before you start: fix Google first

An import copies your Google Business Profile into Bing. It copies the mistakes just as faithfully as the correct details.

So spend ten minutes on Google before you touch Bing. Confirm the business name matches what customers say out loud, the address uses one consistent format, the phone number is the one on your website, hours include holiday exceptions, and the description reads the way you want it to. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/) has the full checklist.

If Google is wrong, everything downstream will be wrong in a second place.

## Step 1: Sign in

Go to Bing Places for Business and sign in. A free Microsoft account works. If several people help run your marketing, use an account the business controls rather than a personal login belonging to someone who might leave.

## Step 2: Search for your business before creating anything

This is the step that prevents the most common mess.

Bing may already have a listing for you, created from public data long before you ever thought about it. Search by business name and location first. If it exists, **claim it** rather than creating a new one. Two listings for the same business split whatever trust signals you have and confuse both the map and the assistants reading it.

If you find a duplicate with old details — a previous address, an old phone number — claim it and correct it or request removal. It is exactly the kind of ghost listing we described in the citation guide.

## Step 3: Import from Google, or enter details manually

If your Google profile is clean, import. Microsoft says its newer import flow is faster and better at preserving details such as business name, hours and contact information.

If your Google profile is messy, or you run a service-area business with unusual address settings, enter details manually instead. It takes longer and removes surprises.

## Step 4: Check every field by hand

Do not click through the review screen. Go line by line:

- **Business name** — identical to Google, character for character, with no keywords appended.
- **Address** — the same format you use everywhere else. Suite numbers placed the same way.
- **Phone** — the number displayed on your website, not a tracking number that appears nowhere else.
- **Category** — check this one twice. Google and Bing do not share an identical category system, so do not assume the right one came across. Pick the closest specific match.
- **Hours** — including any seasonal or holiday differences.
- **Website link** — the correct page, with the right language version if you run a bilingual site.
- **Photos and description** — real photos of the actual premises or work, and a description that says who you serve and where.

## Step 5: Complete verification

Bing offers verification options depending on the business and its details. Complete whichever one you are offered and follow it through.

Two rules while you wait: do not create a second listing because the first one feels slow, and do not change core details mid-verification unless they are genuinely wrong.

## Step 6: Check what is actually showing

Once the listing is live:

1. Search your business name on Bing and on Bing Maps. Confirm the details match.
2. Ask an AI assistant the question a customer would ask in your category and area. See whether you appear and whether the details are right. Ask more than once — answers vary between runs.
3. Write down what you found, with the date.

That third step makes next year's check a comparison rather than a fresh investigation. It is the same audit habit from the [citation map guide](/blog/local-business-citations-guide/), applied to one platform.

## Step 7: Keep two listings accurate by habit

Here is the maintenance rule that matters more than any setting: **treat Google and Bing as two listings you update together.**

Whether an automatic sync is available, and whether it works reliably for your account, is not something to bet your phone number on. Microsoft's own support forums contain plenty of questions from owners whose import or sync did not do what they expected.

So build the habit instead. When hours change for a holiday, when you move, when you add a service or change a phone number, update Google, Bing and Apple Business Connect the same day. Keep a one-line change log. It takes five minutes and prevents the slow drift that leaves a wrong number live somewhere for years.

## The mistakes we see most

- **Creating a new listing instead of claiming the existing one**, leaving two versions of the business live.
- **Importing a messy Google profile**, so the errors now exist in two places.
- **Trusting the category import** and ending up in a vague or wrong category.
- **Using a tracking number** that exists only on one platform.
- **Setting it up once and forgetting it**, then changing hours or phone numbers only on Google.

None of this is difficult. It is just the kind of work that is easy to skip because nobody notices it done — only when it is wrong.
