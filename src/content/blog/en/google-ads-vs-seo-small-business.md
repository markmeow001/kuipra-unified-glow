Every small business hits this fork: a few thousand dollars of marketing budget, and two credible-sounding advisors — one saying "run Google Ads, you'll get calls this week," the other saying "invest in SEO, ads are burning money." Both are half right, and the half they leave out is where budgets go to die.

Here's the honest comparison, and the sequence we actually recommend.

## The core difference: renting vs. building

**Google Ads rents visibility.** You bid, you appear above the organic results, you pay per click. The tap opens in days — and closes the moment you stop paying. Nothing accumulates except your account's learning data.

**SEO builds visibility.** Your Google Business Profile, your site, your content earn rankings over months. Slow to start — and the asset keeps producing after you stop pushing. A page that ranks keeps bringing in searchers without a per-click charge, which is the logic behind [doing local SEO in the right order](/blog/local-seo-guide-vancouver-small-business/).

Neither is morally superior. They're different financial instruments: ads are opex, SEO is capex.

## When ads are the right answer

- **You need leads now** — new business, new location, slow season, spare capacity that costs you money every idle week.
- **The search has buying intent and you can serve it profitably.** "emergency plumber vancouver" at $15/click makes sense when a job is worth $400; it doesn't for a $12 product.
- **You're testing** — which service to push, which message lands, which area responds. Ads are the fastest market-research tool small businesses ignore.
- **The math check that decides everything:** cost per click ÷ your conversion rate = cost per lead. If clicks are $6 and 1 in 10 becomes an inquiry, you're paying $60 per lead — decide if your margins can carry that *before* spending, not after. (Ranges by channel in our [cost guide](/blog/vancouver-marketing-channels-costs/). As an outside cross-check, WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks, drawn from its own customers' campaigns, put the average at $5.42 per click: $2.05 for restaurants, $9.87 for legal ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/)). The report names no region or currency, so it's a sanity check, not a Vancouver price.)

## When SEO is the right answer

- **You'll still be in business in a year.** The single biggest predictor of SEO ROI is simply staying the course long enough to collect it.
- **Your customers research before buying** — clinics, contractors, agencies, anything high-ticket. Educational content compounds trust ads can't buy.
- **Local intent is your bread and butter.** In our experience, for "near me" searches the map pack out-earns paid slots for most local businesses, and it is almost always there: a 2025 Whitespark study of 540 searches in three US cities found local packs on 93% of local-intent queries ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/blog/case-study-the-prevalence-of-ai-overviews-in-local-search/)). Map ranking is built free: complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/), steady [reviews](/blog/google-reviews-management-guide/), [consistent citations](/blog/local-business-citations-guide/).
- **The AI-search bonus:** the same clear, factual content that ranks is what [ChatGPT and AI assistants cite when recommending businesses](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-guide/), and the signals they lean on to pick a local business are largely the ones SEO builds ([how AI assistants choose](/blog/how-ai-assistants-recommend-local-businesses/)). Ads are arriving in AI answers too: Google shows Sponsored ads inside AI Overviews (more below), and OpenAI began testing ads in ChatGPT in the US in February 2026, saying "Ads do not influence the answers ChatGPT gives you" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/10/openai_ads)). But an ad is a labelled slot beside the answer; being the business the answer names still has to be earned.

## What changed on the ads side in 2025–2026

Two Google changes matter for a small budget:

- **Ads now appear inside AI answers.** Google's help documentation lists Canada among the markets where ads can show within AI Overviews and AI Mode, labelled "Sponsored" ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775)). For now that is English only, so Chinese-language searches don't get the in-answer ads; the usual slots above and below the AI Overview still apply. Existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are eligible automatically, and you can't target or opt out of these placements separately. Exact and phrase match keywords qualify only when the search shows direct commercial intent; broad match and AI Max can also be matched to broader, exploratory questions.
- **AI Max widens what your ads match.** AI Max for Search campaigns is an optional layer you switch on inside a regular Search campaign, not a new campaign type. It adds broad-match and "keywordless" search term matching, ad text generated from your ads and website, and final URL expansion, which can send clicks to a different page on your site than the one you chose ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15910187)). Google says advertisers who turn it on "typically see 14% more conversions", based on its own 2025 data ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15910366)).

Our reading for a $500–1,500/month budget: both changes push spend toward broader searches. That can find real customers, but on a small budget it can also spend the month on queries you would never have bid on. Start with exact and phrase match on your proven money terms and read the search terms report every week. If you test AI Max, use its URL exclusions to keep paid clicks off pages that don't convert, such as blog posts.

## The trap in each

**The ads trap:** paying for clicks into a weak website. Google charges worse landing pages more per click: the quality of your ads and landing page is one of the inputs to Ad Rank, and Google says "higher quality ads can often lead to lower CPCs" ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722122)). A site that [fails the conversion basics](/blog/website-conversion-audit-checklist/) turns ad budget into a leak. Fix the bucket before filling it: a [dedicated landing page with working conversion tracking](/blog/landing-page-conversion-tracking-guide/) is what turns the cost-per-lead estimate above into a real number.

**The SEO trap:** paying a retainer for "activity" — reports, meta tweaks, thin blog posts — without strategy. Six months of invoices, nothing compounding. The [filter questions for choosing an agency](/blog/how-to-choose-marketing-agency-vancouver/) apply doubly here.

## The sequence for most Vancouver small businesses

1. **Month 0: free foundation.** Complete GBP, review system running, website passing the conversion audit. Costs discipline, not dollars.
2. **Months 1-3: small, sharp ads.** One campaign, your highest-intent keywords, tight geography, $500-1,500/month. You get leads now *and* real data on which searches convert.
3. **Months 2+: reinvest into content.** Take what ads taught you converts, and build the pages and answers that rank for it organically.
4. **Month 12: rebalance.** As organic leads grow, ads narrow to what organic can't reach — competitive money terms, promotions, new offers. The end state for most local businesses isn't ads *or* SEO; it's a small permanent ads budget on top of an organic base that does the heavy lifting.
