Every small business hits this fork: a few thousand dollars of marketing budget, and two credible-sounding advisors — one saying "run Google Ads, you'll get calls this week," the other saying "invest in SEO, ads are burning money." Both are half right, and the half they leave out is where budgets go to die.

Here's the honest comparison, and the sequence we actually recommend.

## The core difference: renting vs. building

**Google Ads rents visibility.** You bid, you appear above the organic results, you pay per click. The tap opens in days — and closes the moment you stop paying. Nothing accumulates except your account's learning data.

**SEO builds visibility.** Your Google Business Profile, your site, your content earn rankings over months. Slow to start — and the asset keeps producing after you stop pushing. Our clients' oldest articles still generate inquiries [with zero ongoing spend](/blog/local-seo-guide-vancouver-small-business/).

Neither is morally superior. They're different financial instruments: ads are opex, SEO is capex.

## When ads are the right answer

- **You need leads now** — new business, new location, slow season, spare capacity that costs you money every idle week.
- **The search has buying intent and you can serve it profitably.** "emergency plumber vancouver" at $15/click makes sense when a job is worth $400; it doesn't for a $12 product.
- **You're testing** — which service to push, which message lands, which area responds. Ads are the fastest market-research tool small businesses ignore.
- **The math check that decides everything:** cost per click ÷ your conversion rate = cost per lead. If clicks are $6 and 1 in 10 becomes an inquiry, you're paying $60 per lead — decide if your margins can carry that *before* spending, not after. (Ranges by channel in our [cost guide](/blog/vancouver-marketing-channels-costs/).)

## When SEO is the right answer

- **You'll still be in business in a year.** The single biggest predictor of SEO ROI is simply staying the course long enough to collect it.
- **Your customers research before buying** — clinics, contractors, agencies, anything high-ticket. Educational content compounds trust ads can't buy.
- **Local intent is your bread and butter.** For "near me" searches, the map pack out-earns paid slots for most local businesses — and map ranking is built free: complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/), steady [reviews](/blog/google-reviews-management-guide/), consistent citations.
- **The AI-search bonus:** the same clear, factual content that ranks is what [ChatGPT and AI assistants cite when recommending businesses](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-guide/) — a channel ads can't reach at any price.

## The trap in each

**The ads trap:** paying for clicks into a weak website. Google literally charges worse landing pages more per click, and a site that fails the conversion basics turns ad budget into a leak. Fix the bucket before filling it.

**The SEO trap:** paying a retainer for "activity" — reports, meta tweaks, thin blog posts — without strategy. Six months of invoices, nothing compounding. The [filter questions for choosing an agency](/blog/how-to-choose-marketing-agency-vancouver/) apply doubly here.

## The sequence for most Vancouver small businesses

1. **Month 0: free foundation.** Complete GBP, review system running, website passing the conversion audit. Costs discipline, not dollars.
2. **Months 1-3: small, sharp ads.** One campaign, your highest-intent keywords, tight geography, $500-1,500/month. You get leads now *and* real data on which searches convert.
3. **Months 2+: reinvest into content.** Take what ads taught you converts, and build the pages and answers that rank for it organically.
4. **Month 12: rebalance.** As organic leads grow, ads narrow to what organic can't reach — competitive money terms, promotions, new offers. The end state for most local businesses isn't ads *or* SEO; it's a small permanent ads budget on top of an organic base that does the heavy lifting.
