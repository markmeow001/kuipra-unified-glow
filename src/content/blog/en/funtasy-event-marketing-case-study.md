Funtasy Production is one of Greater Vancouver's most active corporate event and seminar producers. Their events regularly draw over a thousand professionals, entrepreneurs, and industry leaders. The events sold out — but online, almost nobody knew they happened.

This is the story of how we fixed that, and the numbers behind it.

## The starting point

When Funtasy came to us, the pattern was familiar: excellent in-person production, underwhelming digital footprint.

- Pre-event buzz was too weak to reach cold audiences, so ticket sales leaned entirely on the existing network.
- Live coverage was reactive — a few photos posted when someone remembered.
- After each event, the momentum simply evaporated. No recap content, no highlight edits, no follow-up narrative.

As their team put it: "In 2025, events that don't live online might as well not exist."

## The framework: before, during, after

We took over full-service social media and event marketing and built a **three-phase system** applied to every activation:

### Phase 1 — Pre-event

Teaser content, speaker spotlights, and countdown sequences designed to make the event feel like a happening *before* doors open. This is where cold audiences enter the funnel.

### Phase 2 — Live coverage

A planned shot list and content roles instead of improvised phone photos. Stories running through the day, strategic reels captured for later editing, and real-time posts timed to peak moments rather than posted after the fact.

### Phase 3 — Post-event amplification

Recap edits, attendee-generated content, and highlight narratives released over the following weeks — converting a one-day event into a month of brand presence and warming up the audience for the next one.

The channel mix followed the audience data: Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn are the dominant discovery channels for event audiences, and influencer partnerships — averaging $5.78 returned per $1 spent across the industry in 2025 — extended reach beyond Funtasy's own following.

## The results

Across the 2025 program — more than a dozen Vancouver activations:

| Metric | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Social media reach growth | **+340%** |
| Event attendees reached per campaign | **1,200+** |
| Average Instagram engagement rate | **8.4%** |
| Vancouver events activated in 2025 | **12+** |

The engagement number is worth pausing on: 8.4% on Instagram is several times typical brand benchmarks, and it came from content strategy, not paid inflation — event content performs when it's structured as a narrative instead of an afterthought.

## What made the difference

Looking back, three decisions did most of the work:

1. **Treating each event as a campaign, not a date.** The three-phase structure means content exists before, during, and after — tripling the surface area of every activation.
2. **Planning live coverage like a production.** Shot lists and role assignments turn chaotic event days into reliable content pipelines.
3. **Building a repeatable system.** Because the framework is the same every time, each event executes faster and performs better than the last.
