Here's where most small-business ad budgets actually die: not in the auction, not in targeting — in the gap between the click and the inquiry. The ad worked. The visitor arrived. The page lost them, and nobody noticed, because nothing was being measured.

This guide covers both halves of the fix: the page that converts, and the tracking that tells you the truth.

## The landing page: one conversation, continued

A landing page has one job — continue the exact conversation the ad started, and end it with one action.

- **Message match, word for word.** Ad says "Emergency Furnace Repair Burnaby"; the headline says the same thing, not "Welcome to our HVAC company." Visitors bounce in seconds when the scent trail breaks — and Google charges more per click for mismatched pages.
- **One action, repeated.** Call, book, or quote — pick one. Navigation menus, social icons and second offers are exits. The best-performing local landing pages are almost embarrassingly simple: headline, proof, form, repeat.
- **Proof near the promise.** Rating stars, review count, real photos, "serving Burnaby since 2015" — the [trust checklist](/blog/website-conversion-audit-checklist/) applies double when the visitor cost you $8 to acquire.
- **Mobile-first, three-second load.** Most paid local clicks are mobile. Every second of load time is paid-for visitors leaving.
- **Bilingual pages for bilingual campaigns.** If you run Chinese-language ads, land them on a Chinese page — [ad-in-Chinese to page-in-English is a broken promise](/blog/marketing-to-vancouver-chinese-community/) that shows up as a high bounce rate you'll misdiagnose as bad targeting.

## The tracking stack: three layers, one afternoon

You can't optimize what you can't see. The minimum honest setup:

1. **GA4 with real events.** Not just pageviews — events on form submits, phone-number taps, booking clicks. These are your "conversions" toggles.
2. **Platform tags.** Google Ads conversion tracking (imported from GA4 or its own tag) and Meta's pixel/CAPI if you run social ads. This is what lets the platforms optimize toward inquiries instead of clicks — the single biggest performance lever most small accounts never pull.
3. **Phone visibility.** If calls are how you close, use a tracking number on the landing page (forwarding to your real line) or at minimum count tel: taps. Half of local conversions are calls; untracked, your cost per lead reads as double reality.

Then run the test almost nobody runs: **submit your own form, call your own number, and verify the conversion appears in GA4 and the ads platform.** Broken tracking fails silently for months — the same way [broken forms do](/blog/website-conversion-audit-checklist/).

## The number that decides everything: true cost per lead

With tracking live, the fog lifts into one number: what does an inquiry actually cost?

**Spend ÷ tracked conversions = cost per lead.** Now decisions make themselves: a $60 CPL is great for a $4,000 reno job and fatal for a $30 product; keyword A produces $25 leads while keyword B produces $180 leads — shift the budget; the Chinese-language campaign converts at half the CPL of the English one — you just learned where your growth is. This is the data that makes the [ads-vs-SEO budget question](/blog/google-ads-vs-seo-small-business/) answerable with arithmetic instead of vibes.

## Iterate like a cheapskate

Don't redesign — test one thing at a time, biggest levers first: the headline (message match), the proof block, the form length. A landing page that converts at 8% instead of 4% just cut your cost per lead in half without touching the ad account. That's the quiet math most agencies skip because media spend is easier to bill.
