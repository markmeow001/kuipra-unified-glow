Before a customer in Vancouver ever visits your website, they've probably already judged you on Google. Search for a dentist in Kitsilano, a ramen spot on Robson, or a plumber in Burnaby, and the first meaningful screen is the local pack: three businesses, their ratings, their photos, their hours. That panel is powered by your Google Business Profile (GBP) — and it's free, which makes it the highest-leverage neglected asset in most local marketing plans we review.

Here's how to run it properly.

## Why GBP matters more than your homepage

For "near me" and neighbourhood-level searches — which skew heavily mobile — Google Maps and the local pack effectively *are* the search results. Many customers call, get directions, or check hours without ever tapping through to a website. If your profile is thin, outdated, or unclaimed, you're invisible at the exact moment someone is ready to buy.

Google ranks local results on three factors: **relevance** (does your profile match the query?), **distance**, and **prominence** (reviews, activity, mentions). You can't move your building, but you control the other two almost entirely through your profile.

## The setup checklist

Work through this once, properly:

### Categories
Your **primary category** is the single strongest relevance signal — choose the most specific one that fits ("Sushi restaurant," not "Restaurant"). Add secondary categories for real secondary offerings, and nothing aspirational.

### Service areas and address
Storefronts show an address. Service businesses (contractors, cleaners, mobile services) should hide the address and define service areas — Vancouver, Burnaby, Richmond, wherever you genuinely serve. Don't list half of BC to look bigger; it dilutes relevance.

### Hours
Set regular hours, then set **special hours for every holiday** — BC has a lot of them. In your profile, go to Edit profile > Hours and edit Special hours. Google's own guidance is to confirm hours for official holidays even when they match your regular hours, and to mark the business "Temporarily closed" if you will be shut for seven days or more ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076)). "Closed when Google said open" is one of the fastest ways to earn a one-star review. Make the same change on [Bing Places](/blog/bing-places-setup-guide/) and Apple Maps the same day, because customers check more than one map.

### Attributes and services
Fill in every applicable attribute: wheelchair accessible, patio, women-owned, languages spoken, payment types. Then list your actual services or menu items with descriptions. Each field is another query you can match.

## Photo strategy

Profiles with real, current photos earn more clicks and direction requests than profiles running on stock images or three-year-old shots. Cover four angles: **exterior** (so people recognize you from the street), **interior** (so they know what to expect), **products or work**, and **team**. Upload a few new ones monthly — recency is itself a signal, to both Google and humans.

## Reviews: the engine of prominence

Reviews are the loudest prominence signal and the first thing customers actually read.

**Collecting:** Ask every customer at a natural point — after the job wraps, at pickup, in the follow-up email. Use your short review link or a QR code at the counter. Then stay inside Google's rules, which were tightened in 2026 ([review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)):

- **No incentives** of any kind (payment, discounts, free goods or services) in exchange for a review.
- **No review gating**: you can't ask only the customers you expect to be happy.
- **No pressure, no scripts**: don't pressure people to review while they are on your premises, and don't ask them to include specific content. Ask, then leave the wording to them.

Google enforces this actively. Its penalties include blocking new reviews for a set period and showing customers a warning that fake reviews were removed ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287)). In Canada there is a second layer: the Competition Bureau has warned that anyone who writes or permits reviews that give a false or misleading impression could be liable, and that employees must disclose their connection when they review ([Competition Bureau](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2024/01/online-reviews-posted-by-employees-businesses-could-be-liable.html)). Our [Google reviews guide](/blog/google-reviews-management-guide/) covers how to build reviews steadily within these rules. One exception to all of the above: if you're a regulated health professional, your college's rules come first. Since April 1, 2026, BC's College of Complementary Health Professionals (chiropractic, massage therapy, naturopathic medicine, TCM and acupuncture) says licensees must not ask patients for reviews at all, including by giving them a link ([CCHPBC FAQ](https://cchpbc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/FAQAdverting.Marketing.V3.FINAL_.pdf)); our [clinic marketing guide](/blog/clinic-marketing-guide-vancouver/) covers the details.

**Replying:** Reply to every review, positive and negative. For positive reviews, a specific two-line thank-you beats a copy-paste template. For negative ones: stay calm, acknowledge the specific issue, never argue publicly, and move resolution offline with a real contact. Prospective customers judge you far more on how you handle criticism than on the criticism itself.

## Google Posts, and what happened to Q&A

**Posts** are free micro-ads on your profile — offers, events, new products, seasonal updates. One post a week or two a month keeps the profile visibly alive.

The **Q&A section** this guide originally told you to seed is on its way out. In December 2025, Google confirmed it was removing Q&A from Google Maps in favour of an AI-powered "Ask" feature ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-maps-qa-feature-ask-40594.html)). As of early 2026, industry trackers reported that feature only in the US and India ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-q-and-a/)), so Canadian owners may still see older questions in their dashboard, where they can answer them.

The practical takeaway is the same either way: the answers to your most common questions (parking, reservations, languages spoken, pricing basics) need to live somewhere both people and AI can read them. Put them in your services list, your profile description and an FAQ section on your own website.

## The mistakes we see constantly

1. **Keyword-stuffed business names.** "Joe's Plumbing | Best Emergency Plumber Vancouver BC" breaks Google's rules. Your name should match the real-world name on your storefront, website and stationery, and Google's guidelines state plainly that "including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted, and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile" ([Google guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Keywords belong in categories and services.
2. **Ignored reviews.** A wall of unanswered reviews reads as a business that doesn't care.
3. **Stale hours and photos.** Wrong holiday hours actively destroy trust.
4. **Set-and-forget.** A profile that hasn't changed in a year loses momentum against competitors who post, reply, and update.

## What else changed in 2025–2026

- **Verification.** Google decides which methods you are offered (phone or text, email, live video call or mail) and recommends video when you are eligible ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242)).
- **Review enforcement.** Google says it blocked or removed more than 292 million policy-violating reviews in 2025, and that when it detects a spike in fake reviews it removes them, pauses new reviews and alerts the owner ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/)).
- **Review extortion.** If someone threatens a wave of bad reviews unless you pay, Google's advice is direct: "Do not engage with or pay the malicious individuals," and report it through its help page ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16404809)).
- **US-first features.** Several new features, including the AI "Ask" button in Maps, launched in the US first. Check your own dashboard before assuming an American article applies to a Vancouver business.

## GBP now feeds AI recommendations too

Here's the part most local businesses haven't clocked yet: when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, or an AI-powered search for "a good family dentist near Metrotown," those systems draw on the same signals — your categories, reviews, ratings, and consistency across the web. Google's own guidance on AI search says Business Profiles "can help your products and services to be visible in both AI responses and other Google Search results" ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).
