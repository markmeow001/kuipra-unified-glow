Search for "small business grants BC" in English or Chinese and you'll find a graveyard that still looks like a garden: page after page recommending grants that closed years ago. Several popular guides in Vancouver's Chinese-language business community still circulate application walkthroughs for programs that stopped taking applications in 2021–2024.

So let's do this properly: what's dead, what's actually open as of November 2026 (we re-checked every grant program below against its official page on November 6, 2026), and how a small business should think about the whole category. One disclaimer up front: **program rules change; verify everything against the official government pages before making plans.**

## The dead list (stop chasing these)

- **CDAP — Boost Your Business Technology.** The $15,000 digital-plan grant with the 0% BDC loan up to $100,000. It stopped taking new applications on [February 19, 2024](https://www.bdc.ca/en/canada-digital-adoption-program), and ISED's [2024–25 reporting](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/planning-performance-reporting/en/departmental-results-reports/iseds-supplementary-information-tables-2024-25) lists the program's end date as March 31, 2025. Its old program page now just forwards to ISED's homepage. No federal successor has been announced.
- **CDAP — Grow Your Business Online.** The famous $2,400 e-commerce micro-grant, delivered through Digital Main Street and others. Closed, and it ended with the rest of CDAP on March 31, 2025.
- **Launch Online Grant (BC).** The 2021 pandemic-era program that covered up to 75% of e-commerce build costs, to $7,500. Its [own site](https://www.launchonline.ca) says intake is closed. It is still widely cited in older Chinese-language guides.
- **Small Business BC's grant listings.** The non-profit that many older guides send you to [shut down in December 2024](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/small-business-bc-bankruptcy-creditors-claims). Its web address now forwards to the Province's [small business page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/small-business).

If a marketing agency or consultant is dangling any of these in 2026 to close a sale, that tells you what you need to know about them.

## What's actually open

### B.C. Employer Training Grant: the sleeper for marketing skills

WorkBC's most recent notice (March 23, 2026) says the [B.C. Employer Training Grant](https://www.workbc.ca/employers-industry/funding-programs/bc-employer-training-grant) is open for the 2026/27 fiscal year, for training that starts on or after April 1, 2026, and there was no closure notice when we checked in November. It reimburses up to **80% of eligible training costs, to $10,000 per employee** (annual employer maximum $300,000), and small businesses are one of six priority factors.

Here's the marketing angle most owners miss: it funds *training*, and digital marketing skills are trainable. Courses in digital marketing, analytics, e-commerce operations or design for you or your staff can qualify where the training meets program rules. The grant won't pay an agency to run your ads, but it can subsidize turning someone on your team into the person who runs them. (Decide which channel first; our [Google Ads vs. SEO comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-seo-small-business/) helps, so the course matches the work you'll actually do.)

The rules that trip people up, from WorkBC's [program page and FAQ](https://www.workbc.ca/employers-industry/funding-programs/bc-employer-training-grant/frequently-asked-questions):

- **Apply before the training starts.** Courses already underway aren't funded.
- **The trainer must be an eligible B.C.-based third party.** Training your own staff deliver doesn't count, and diploma or degree programs aren't funded.
- **Participants must be Canadian citizens, permanent residents or protected persons.** Staff on work permits or study permits don't qualify.
- **Owners count.** A self-employed owner can apply and be the participant.

Training through a B.C. public post-secondary institution earns an extra priority point in 2026/27. Funding is limited, and lower-priority applications may be returned unassessed; in 2024/25 the budget was fully allocated by early December. So apply as early as you can, and don't count on money late in the fiscal year.

### CanExport SMEs: if you sell beyond Canada (currently between intakes)

As of November 2026, the [CanExport SMEs page](https://www.tradecommissioner.gc.ca/en/our-solutions/funding-financing-international-business/canexport-smes.html) says the program is closed: all applications for the 2026–27 intake have been processed, and future intakes will be announced on that page.

When it is open, it covers up to **50% of eligible export-development costs, to $50,000 per project**. The [2026–27 applicant's guide](https://www.tradecommissioner.gc.ca/en/our-solutions/funding-financing-international-business/canexport-smes/applicants-guide-2026-27.html) requires an incorporated company (or LLP or co-operative) with **3 to 500 full-time employees and $300,000 to $100 million in annual revenue**, up from the old 1-FTE/$100K thresholds. A project can target the U.S. or other international markets, but [not both](https://www.tradecommissioner.gc.ca/en/our-solutions/funding-financing-international-business/canexport-smes/new-2026-2027.html), and agri-food and seafood companies now go through a separate AgriMarketing program.

The marketing catch: the 2026–27 guide **excludes advertising, including online and social media ads, and SEO**. What it does cover is closer to market entry: exhibiting at trade events in your target market, creating or translating promotional materials, translating or adapting your website, and market research by an eligible consultant. It's no longer a micro-business program and it won't fund an ad campaign, but for a product company eyeing the US or Asia it's still worth watching for the next intake.

### Worth a look, depending on your shape

- **Creative Export Canada.** Canadian Heritage's export fund for creative-industry companies. Both 2026–27 intakes have closed; the [program page](https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/services/funding/creative-export-canada.html) says the next period, for expenses from April 1, 2027, will be announced in fall 2026.
- **NRC IRAP and SR&ED.** For genuine technology development, not marketing, but often adjacent when businesses build custom digital products. IRAP is still run by the NRC; [its page](https://nrc.canada.ca/en/support-technology-innovation) notes it will move into a new Canada Innovation Corporation no later than 2026–27.
- **Sector and municipal programs.** Tourism, agrifood and downtown-revitalization streams appear and vanish. Check the federal [Business Benefits Finder](https://innovation.canada.ca/) and the Province's small business page a couple of times a year rather than assuming.

### New since 2025, but probably not for a local shop

Two newer federal programs delivered in B.C. by PacifiCan show up in grant roundups. Neither is a marketing grant for a typical local business:

- **Regional Tariff Response Initiative (RTRI).** Accepting applications, and its "pivot projects" can include market diversification and export development. But the [eligibility page](https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/services/funding/regional-tariff-response-initiative/rtri-eligible-applicants.html) requires an incorporated B.C. business with at least $1 million in annual revenue that can show tariff impact, and it says retail businesses and local consumer services, such as restaurants, are generally ineligible.
- **Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative (RAII).** [Repayable contributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative.html) of $200,000 to $5 million per project; AI-adoption projects need 5 to 500 full-time-equivalent staff and three years operating in B.C. The current intake closes November 23, 2026.

Canada's national AI strategy, last updated June 2026, also points small businesses toward financing and self-assessment tools rather than grants. Bottom line as of November 2026: we found no new federal or B.C. grant that pays for a small business's website, ads or social media.

## How to think about grants now

The honest 2026 picture: **direct subsidies for websites, ads and everyday marketing are gone.** What remains funds *capability* (training) and *expansion* (export). Four practical rules:

1. **Never build the plan around the grant.** Marketing that only makes sense when subsidized doesn't make sense. Decide what's worth doing at full price (our [channel-by-channel cost guide](/blog/vancouver-marketing-channels-costs/) helps), then check whether a program happens to defray part of it.
2. **Start with what costs time, not money.** The highest-return basics for most local businesses were never grant-dependent: a complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/), a website that [passes the conversion basics](/blog/website-conversion-audit-checklist/), and an [email list you own](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/).
3. **Date-check everything you read**, including this article. If a guide doesn't mention CDAP's closure, or still sends you to Small Business BC, assume the rest is stale too.
4. **Applications cost real hours.** An afternoon for a training-grant claim is fine; weeks chasing a long-shot program is marketing time better spent marketing.
