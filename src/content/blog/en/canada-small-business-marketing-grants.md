Search for "small business grants BC" in English or Chinese and you'll find a graveyard that still looks like a garden: page after page recommending grants that closed years ago. Several popular guides in Vancouver's Chinese-language business community still circulate application walkthroughs for programs that stopped taking applications in 2021–2024.

So let's do this properly: what's dead, what's actually open as of September 2026, and how a small business should think about the whole category. One disclaimer up front — **program rules change; verify everything against the official government pages before making plans.**

## The dead list (stop chasing these)

- **CDAP — Boost Your Business Technology.** The $15,000 digital-plan grant with the 0% BDC loan up to $100,000. Closed to new applications since February 2024; only businesses with agreements signed before then are still being processed. No federal successor has been announced.
- **CDAP — Grow Your Business Online.** The famous $2,400 e-commerce micro-grant, delivered through Digital Main Street and others. Closed nationally.
- **Launch Online Grant (BC).** The pandemic-era program that covered up to $7,500 of e-commerce builds. Ended years ago, still widely cited in older Chinese-language guides.

If a marketing agency or consultant is dangling any of these in 2026 to close a sale, that tells you what you need to know about them.

## What's actually open

### B.C. Employer Training Grant — the sleeper for marketing skills

Open for the 2026/27 fiscal year (training starting on or after April 1, 2026), the [B.C. Employer Training Grant](https://www.workbc.ca/find-loans-and-grants/industry-and-employers/bc-employer-training-grant) reimburses up to **80% of eligible training costs, to $10,000 per employee** (annual employer maximum $300,000), with small businesses given priority.

Here's the marketing angle most owners miss: it funds *training* — and digital marketing skills are trainable. Courses in digital marketing, analytics, e-commerce operations or design for you or your staff can qualify where the training meets program rules. The grant won't pay an agency to run your ads — but it can subsidize turning someone on your team into the person who runs them. Training through BC public post-secondary institutions gets extra priority in 2026.

### CanExport SMEs — if you sell beyond Canada

For incorporated businesses expanding into international markets, [CanExport SMEs](https://www.tradecommissioner.gc.ca/en/funding-support-programs/canexport/sme/canexport-sme.html) covers up to **50% of eligible export-development costs, to $50,000 per project** — trade shows, market research, translation and adaptation of marketing materials, international ad campaigns.

Note the tightened 2026–27 eligibility: roughly **3 full-time employees and $300,000+ in revenue** (up from the old 1-FTE/$100K thresholds). It's no longer a micro-business program — but for a product company eyeing the US or Asia, it's the most generous marketing-adjacent money still on the table.

### Worth a look, depending on your shape

- **Creative Export Canada** — significant cost-share for creative-industry companies with international traction.
- **NRC IRAP and SR&ED** — for genuine technology development, not marketing, but often adjacent when businesses build custom digital products.
- **Sector and municipal programs** — tourism, agrifood and downtown-revitalization streams appear and vanish; check Small Business BC's current listings a couple of times a year rather than assuming.

## How to think about grants now

The honest 2026 picture: **direct subsidies for websites, ads and everyday marketing are gone.** What remains funds *capability* (training) and *expansion* (export). Three practical rules:

1. **Never build the plan around the grant.** Marketing that only makes sense when subsidized doesn't make sense — decide what's worth doing at full price (our [channel-by-channel cost guide](/blog/vancouver-marketing-channels-costs/) helps), then check whether a program happens to defray part of it.
2. **Date-check everything you read**, including this article. If a guide doesn't show the closure of CDAP, it predates 2024 — assume the rest is stale too.
3. **Applications cost real hours.** An afternoon for a training-grant claim is fine; weeks chasing a long-shot program is marketing time better spent marketing.
