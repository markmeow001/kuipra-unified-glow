Ask most business owners about Google reviews and they'll quote you one number: their star rating. But that's not how customers — or Google — actually read your reviews. A 4.9 built on twelve reviews from 2023 loses to a 4.6 with fresh, detailed reviews arriving every week. Recency and substance carry the weight, and both are things you can influence this month without spending a dollar or breaking a single rule.

Here's the system we use.

## Recency and substance beat raw stars

Customers instinctively check the newest reviews first — they want to know what your business is like *now*, not two owners ago. Google reads reviews the same way: a steady stream of recent, specific reviews is a stronger prominence signal than a large but dormant pile.

Substance matters just as much. "Great place!" tells Google and future customers almost nothing. "The team repaired our furnace the same day and explained the invoice line by line" is a small piece of marketing copy you didn't have to write — full of the words people actually search for. Your goal isn't just more reviews; it's more reviews that say something.

## How to ask — the legitimate way

Most happy customers will leave a review if you ask at the right moment and make it effortless.

### Time the ask at the moment of delight
Ask when satisfaction peaks: the job just wrapped, the compliment just landed, the problem just got solved. A day later, the warm feeling has faded and so has your response rate.

### Put a QR code at the point of service
A QR code linking to your review form — on the counter, the invoice, the packaging — removes every step between "I'm happy" and "I said so publicly." Use the short review link from your Business Profile.

### Follow up, once
A brief message the next day — "Thanks for choosing us. If we did a good job, a Google review helps us more than you'd think" — works because it's personal and low-pressure. One follow-up. Nagging turns goodwill into annoyance.

### What's against the rules
Google's policy is blunt, and violations risk review removal or worse:

- **Incentivized reviews.** No discounts, gifts, or contest entries for reviews — even honest ones.
- **Review gating.** Surveying customers first and only sending the happy ones to Google is explicitly prohibited.
- **Buying reviews.** Purchased reviews are policy violations, often legally problematic, and usually obvious to readers anyway.

Ask everyone, reward no one, filter nothing.

## Replying to positive reviews without sounding robotic

Reply to every positive review — it signals to reviewers, prospects, and Google that someone is home. The trap is the template. Ten identical "Thank you for your kind words!" replies read worse than silence.

The fix is simple: reference one specific thing from their review. They mentioned the patio? Mention the patio back. Two sentences, written like a person, signed off like a person. Thirty seconds per review is the entire cost.

## The negative review playbook

A negative review isn't a crisis; a badly handled one is. Prospective customers judge you far more on how you respond than on the complaint itself.

1. **Respond within a day or two.** Speed shows attentiveness. But never reply angry — if the review stings, draft it, walk away, and post after you've cooled off.
2. **Acknowledge the specifics.** "We're sorry the Saturday wait ran past thirty minutes" beats a generic apology, because it proves you actually read the complaint.
3. **Take it offline.** Offer a real name and a real contact method. The public reply shows willingness; the resolution happens in private.
4. **Never argue publicly.** Even when the reviewer is wrong, a defensive back-and-forth makes you look worse to every future reader. State your side once, factually and calmly, then stop.
5. **Flag genuine fakes.** If a review is spam, from a competitor, or from someone who was never a customer, flag it through your profile and reply publicly with a calm factual note in the meantime. Reserve flagging for real policy violations — not honest criticism you don't enjoy.

## Reviews feed your local SEO — and AI recommendations

Every detailed review adds searchable words to your profile: services, neighbourhoods, product names. When someone searches "emergency plumber East Vancouver," reviews containing those words help you match the query — this is a core input to the local ranking we covered in our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-vancouver/). And when someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini for a recommendation, those systems lean on the same signals: volume, recency, sentiment, and detail. Substantive reviews are becoming the raw material of AI answers.

## Your weekly review-ops routine

This takes fifteen minutes a week:

- **Monday:** Read new reviews. Reply to every one — specific thanks for positives, the playbook for negatives.
- **Midweek:** Identify this week's happiest customers and ask them, personally, at the moment of delight.
- **Friday:** Check that your review QR code and short link still work, and skim the month's reviews for recurring themes — free operational feedback.

Small, boring, consistent. That's what a durable review base is built on.
