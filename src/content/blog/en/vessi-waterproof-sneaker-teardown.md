Most product claims ask you to take the brand's word for it. "Comfortable." "Premium." "Built to last." You find out whether they are true weeks later, if ever.

Vessi's claim is different. Its shoes are waterproof, and you can check that in about the time it takes to turn on a tap.

That one fact explains a surprising amount about how this Vancouver brand grew, and also where it has had to work hardest.

The usual disclosure: **Vessi is not a Kuipra client.** This is an outside teardown built from the company's own announcements, its Kickstarter pages and interviews its leaders have given to the press. Sales figures below are company statements, not audited numbers.

The facts. Vessi has three co-founders, Andy Wang, Tony Yu and Mikaella Go, according to a [2019 Daily Hive interview](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/mikaella-go-vessi-footwear) with Go. Vessi's [own timeline](https://vessi.com/pages/our-story) says its waterproof Dyma-tex material was invented by Wang in 2016. The brand launched on Kickstarter in late 2017, opened its first store at Metrotown in Burnaby in 2022 and its first U.S. store in Bellevue, Washington, in [January 2026](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/vessi-opens-first-u-s-retail-store-at-bellevue-square-874534216.html). In December 2025, Wang told [Business in Vancouver](https://www.biv.com/news/retail-manufacturing/bcs-waterproof-shoe-innovator-vessi-nears-100m-sales-target-11633245) that annual sales have exceeded $90 million every year since 2023, and the company calls itself bootstrapped.

## A claim you can prove in seconds

Look at how customers talked about the product early on. In 2020, [Strategy](https://strategyonline.ca/2020/08/17/vessi-tries-on-a-new-approach-with-ar/) described customers filming themselves "running the shoes under a faucet or walking across a stream."

That is the marketing working at its best: customers producing the proof themselves, unprompted, because the claim is so easy to test. Nobody films themselves verifying that a sneaker is "premium."

The same logic shows up in the stores. Daily Hive's tour of the [Metrotown store](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/inside-vessi-first-ever-vancouver-store) noted its interactive water features, and Wang told [Retail Insider](https://retail-insider.com/retail-insider/2024/11/vessi-expands-with-new-stores-across-canada/) in 2024 that customers "step into a water bucket." The store is built around the demo.

For a small business, the question is not "are we waterproof?" It is "what is the one thing we claim that a customer could check for themselves in under a minute?" A cleaner who lets you run a white glove along the shelf. A mattress shop with a glass of water on the bed. A café that grinds the beans in front of you. If you can find that claim, your customers can do your marketing for you, and our [short-form video playbook](/blog/short-form-video-guide-vancouver/) covers how to film it without a studio.

## Pre-sell before you build

Vessi's first [Kickstarter campaign](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/vessifootwear/vessi-the-worlds-first-100-waterproof-knit-shoes) asked for US$20,000. It raised US$1,026,231 from 7,654 backers between November 2017 and January 2018. A [second campaign](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/vessifootwear/vessi-the-100-waterproof-extremely-breathable-knit) a year later raised another US$1.26 million from 9,365 backers.

Crowdfunding did three jobs at once. It funded production without outside investors. It proved demand with real money rather than survey answers. And it built a first community of customers who had a stake in the brand before it shipped a single pair.

Even established brands keep using it: Vessi went back to Kickstarter in 2022 for a [high-top model](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/vessifootwear/the-cloudburst-high-top-by-vessi), which raised US$849,314.

You do not need Kickstarter to borrow the idea. Pre-orders for a new menu item, a deposit list for a new service, a waitlist for a class: anything that asks customers to commit before you invest tells you more than a poll ever will.

## The cost of renting your audience

Vessi has never hidden how it grows. In 2020, co-founder Tony Yu told [Strategy](https://strategyonline.ca/2020/04/27/how-vessis-relief-for-frontline-workers-tripled-sales/) the company was "pretty heavy" on paid advertising. In March 2026, its director of ecommerce told [Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/how-vessi-sells-waterproof-shoes), "We invest heavily in Meta for customer acquisition." In the December 2025 BIV piece, Wang called digital marketing a "huge pillar," and the company put e-commerce at about 87% of sales.

Paid social clearly worked for Vessi. But it carries a structural risk every small business should understand: you are renting attention from a platform whose prices, rules and algorithms you do not control.

Vessi felt that early. When the pandemic hit in 2020, Strategy reported an "immediate downturn." Its response was unusual: it moved advertising budget into giving shoes to frontline workers, and the company says sales tripled. Whether or not you could repeat that, the lesson is that a brand built mostly on paid reach has to keep finding new ways to earn attention when the usual channel stops performing.

The stores read partly as an answer to that risk. Wang told Retail Insider he sees them as "a marketing channel," a place where the demo happens in person. The [Lush teardown](/blog/lush-social-media-exit-teardown/) looks at the extreme version of reducing platform dependence, and our [email marketing guide](/blog/email-marketing-for-local-businesses/) covers the channel you actually own.

## A claim does not stretch forever

The strength of a single, testable claim is also its limit. Practical Ecommerce reports that when Vessi added apparel, it "performed poorly and diverted resources."

A waterproof shoe has an obvious reason to exist. A waterproof-shoe brand's t-shirt does not inherit that reason automatically. More recently, Vessi has invested in its brand more broadly, with a [2025 rebrand](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/vessi-known-for-waterproof-comfort-unveils-a-new-brand-embracing-rain-and-water-magic-812108567.html) and a [new chief marketing officer](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/vessi-appoints-lorrin-pascoe-as-chief-marketing-officer-to-accelerate-global-brand-growth-813055334.html) the same year.

For a small business, the practical version is: when you add a product or service, ask whether your core claim still applies to it. If it does not, you are starting from zero, and you should budget as if you were.

## Three moves you can steal at any size

**Find the claim a customer can test in under a minute.** Then make the test easy: in the store, on your website, in your videos. Let customers prove it for you.

**Pre-sell before you invest.** Deposits, pre-orders and waitlists tell you what people will pay for, and they fund the first run.

**Do not rent your whole audience.** Use paid social if it works, but keep building something you own: an email list, regulars, a physical place where the proof happens.

Vessi did not need customers to trust a slogan. It gave them a tap and a pair of shoes, and let them see for themselves.
